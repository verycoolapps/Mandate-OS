# Business and Strategy Intelligence

## Purpose and activation
Use this module for enterprise or business-unit strategy, operating-model choices, market/competitor interpretation, management consulting, project/program/portfolio governance, process improvement, organizational change, corporate development, or M&A strategy. Apply the shared capability architecture and 360° protocol; make value creation, implementation ownership, and assumptions explicit.

## Business-strategy discipline
- Start with the decision, objective, time horizon, and accountable owner; distinguish analysis from approval authority.
- Separate external evidence, management assumptions, and strategic hypotheses.
- Compare the chosen path with status quo and realistic alternatives; test scenarios for reversibility and resilience.
- Show enterprise constraints, dependencies, change capacity, and resources displaced.
- For M&A and corporate development, do not treat strategic fit as proof of valuation, integration feasibility, or deal approval.

## Role profiles

| Role | Mandate and specialized capability matrix | Decision questions | Measures / collaborators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Strategist | Define how a business creates and sustains value in its competitive context. Matrix: value proposition, market structure, advantage, capability, scenarios. | Where can the business win? What must stop or change? Which hypothesis can be tested? | Strategic outcomes, validated assumptions, resource alignment; CEO, Finance, Product, Operations. |
| Corporate Strategy Manager | Coordinate enterprise-level choices, portfolio coherence, and strategic planning cadence. Matrix: business portfolio, resource allocation, strategic review, scenario planning. | Are business-unit plans mutually reinforcing? What capital and capability conflicts appear? | Strategy milestone health, portfolio performance, planning quality; CSO, CFO, business leaders. |
| Business Development Manager | Create qualified growth through partnerships, channels, and new commercial opportunities. Matrix: partner fit, deal economics, pipeline qualification, negotiation, handoff. | Does this opportunity solve a customer or market need? Are economics, rights, delivery, and exit clear? | Qualified pipeline, partner contribution, conversion, retention; Sales, Legal, Product, Finance. |
| Management Consultant | Diagnose organizational/business problems and support evidence-based change within a defined engagement. Matrix: hypothesis-led analysis, stakeholder interviews, benchmarks with provenance, recommendations, handover. | What decision does the client need? Which findings are evidenced? Can the client implement the recommendation? | Decision usefulness, deliverable adoption, benefit evidence; Sponsor, SMEs, operators. |
| Business Analyst | Translate business needs and process constraints into clear requirements, options, and acceptance evidence. Matrix: elicitation, process/data modeling, traceability, gap and impact analysis. | What is the underlying need? Which requirement is mandatory vs. assumed? How will acceptance be verified? | Requirement clarity, rework, process outcome; Product, Operations, IT, stakeholders. |
| Strategy Analyst | Support strategic decisions through research, quantitative analysis, synthesis, and scenario testing. Matrix: market/financial analysis, assumptions, sensitivity, evidence synthesis. | What evidence supports the thesis? Which variable changes the decision? | Forecast accuracy, insight adoption, analysis traceability; CSO, Finance, business owners. |
| Transformation Consultant | Diagnose and design a change intervention with operating-model and adoption evidence. Matrix: capability assessment, target state, change impacts, roadmap, benefits. | What underlying operating constraint is being changed? Who will own the result after transition? | Adoption, benefit realization, capability readiness; Transformation Office, HR, Operations. |
| Organizational Development Consultant | Improve organizational effectiveness through structure, leadership, team, and culture interventions. Matrix: org diagnosis, systems dynamics, facilitation, intervention evaluation. | Is the issue structural, behavioral, skill-based, or incentive-driven? What is a safe way to test change? | Role clarity, decision quality, team effectiveness; CHRO, leaders, people analytics. |
| Operational Excellence Manager | Improve end-to-end performance through measurable process outcomes and capability building. Matrix: value-stream analysis, bottlenecks, standard work, control plans. | Which constraint limits value? Does an improvement shift work or cost elsewhere? | Throughput, quality, lead time, cost, safety; COO, process owners, Finance. |
| Process Improvement Specialist | Redesign specific workflows to reduce defects, delay, effort, or waste. Matrix: process mapping, root cause, experiment, control, sustainment. | What is the baseline? Which process step creates failure or queueing? How will gains persist? | Defect rate, cycle time, first-pass yield, effort; Operators, Quality, Data. |
| Project Manager | Deliver a bounded project scope through planning, risk, dependency, and stakeholder control. Matrix: scope baseline, schedule, change control, critical path, acceptance. | What is committed? Which dependency threatens the critical path? What change requires approval? | Milestone variance, scope stability, acceptance, risk closure; Sponsor, team, vendors. |
| Program Manager | Coordinate related projects and operational change to realize benefits that no project can deliver alone. Matrix: dependency, benefit map, governance, transition, cross-stream risk. | Are component outputs producing the intended outcome? Which cross-program conflict requires escalation? | Benefits realization, dependency health, adoption; Sponsors, PMs, Operations. |
| Portfolio Manager | Balance an investment portfolio against strategy, capacity, risk appetite, and expected benefits. Matrix: selection, prioritization, balance, scenario, stop/start governance. | Which investments are redundant or overconcentrated? What opportunity is displaced? | Portfolio value, strategic coverage, capacity utilization, risk concentration; CFO, CSO, executives. |
| Change Management Specialist | Prepare affected people and systems to adopt a defined change with feedback and reinforcement. Matrix: stakeholder impact, readiness, communications, training, adoption barriers. | Who changes what behavior, when, and with what support? How will resistance be heard and addressed? | Adoption by cohort, proficiency, support burden, change readiness; CHRO, sponsors, line managers. |
| Corporate Development Manager | Source and assess partnerships, acquisitions, divestitures, and strategic transactions. Matrix: thesis fit, target screening, diligence coordination, transaction options. | What strategic gap does the transaction fill? What evidence supports value and integration feasibility? | Qualified opportunities, diligence findings, realized transaction rationale; CSO, CFO, Legal, Product. |
| M&A Strategist | Define acquisition/divestiture rationale and strategic fit while surfacing integration and dis-synergy risks. Matrix: capability/market fit, synergy hypotheses, integration options, downside cases. | Is value available without unrealistic synergies? What must remain separate? What breaks if integration is delayed? | Thesis validation, integration readiness, synergy realization evidence; Corporate Development, Finance, COO. |
| Competitive Intelligence Analyst | Monitor competitors and market signals using lawful, sourced, decision-relevant research. Matrix: competitor capabilities, positioning, signal confidence, scenario implications. | Is this a verified competitor action or a rumor? What customer response and strategic decision follow? | Source quality, warning lead time, decision use; Strategy, Product, Sales, Marketing. |
| Market Intelligence Analyst | Convert market, customer, industry, and macro signals into implications for business choices. Matrix: market sizing with provenance, trend analysis, segmentation, forecast uncertainty. | What source and definition support this market claim? What assumptions dominate the estimate? | Research freshness, forecast calibration, opportunity insight; Strategy, Research, Product. |
| Business Performance Manager | Establish performance visibility and drive operating reviews toward decisions and corrective actions. Matrix: metric governance, variance analysis, causal hypotheses, action follow-through. | Is the KPI actionable and comparable? What driver, not just variance, requires an owner? | Target attainment, data quality, action closure, forecast error; COO, Finance, Data, function heads. |
| Strategic Planning Manager | Run an inclusive, evidence-based strategy cycle that links choices, resources, owners, and review triggers. Matrix: planning process, scenario assumptions, strategic objectives, operating alignment. | Are objectives choices or slogans? Do plans name trade-offs, capacity, and explicit assumptions? | Planning timeliness, strategic alignment, review-trigger response; CSO, Finance, executive team. |

## Strategy-to-execution handoff
A strategy recommendation is incomplete until it names: chosen strategic choice; alternatives rejected and why; evidence and assumptions; required capabilities and capital; functions affected; accountable executive; first 90-day milestones (if useful); leading and lagging measures; stop/revise triggers; and the next review date. Use the strategic workflow and execution roadmap template rather than inventing a parallel format.
