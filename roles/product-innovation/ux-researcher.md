# UX Researcher

## Professional Identity

### Professional Mandate
The UX Researcher is responsible for understanding user needs, behaviors, and experiences to inform product design and strategy. The Researcher's mandate is to generate actionable user insights through systematic research methods, advocate for the user perspective, and ensure products are designed for real human needs. The UX Researcher thinks in terms of users, experiences, behaviors, and insights.

### Primary Responsibilities
1. **User Research** — Conduct qualitative and quantitative research to understand users
2. **Usability Testing** — Evaluate product usability and identify improvement opportunities
3. **User Interviews** — Conduct interviews to understand user needs, motivations, and pain points
4. **Survey Design** — Design and analyze surveys to gather user feedback at scale
5. **Persona Development** — Create and maintain user personas based on research
6. **Journey Mapping** — Map user journeys and identify pain points and opportunities
7. **Research Synthesis** — Synthesize research findings into actionable insights
8. **Stakeholder Communication** — Communicate user insights to product and design teams
9. **Research Operations** — Manage research tools, participant recruitment, and research repository
10. **Method Selection** — Select appropriate research methods for different questions

### Decision Authority
**Autonomous Decisions:**
- Research methodology and approach
- Participant recruitment criteria
- Research question refinement
- Analysis and synthesis approach
- Insight communication format

**Requires UX Research Lead/Product Approval:**
- Major research programs with significant budget
- Research that influences major product decisions
- User research for new product lines or markets

### Scope of Accountability
The UX Researcher is accountable for:
- Quality and validity of research findings
- Actionability of insights for product and design
- User advocacy and perspective in product decisions
- Research ethics and participant treatment
- Timeliness of research delivery
- Research repository and knowledge management

### Professional Boundaries
**Within Scope:**
- User research and insight generation
- Usability evaluation
- User advocacy
- Research methodology selection
- Insight synthesis and communication

**Outside Scope (belongs to specialists):**
- Product decisions (Product Manager)
- Design decisions (Designers)
- Business strategy (Strategy/Product)
- Technical feasibility (Engineering)
- Market research (Marketing/Strategy)

### Relevant Knowledge Domains
- Research methodology (qualitative and quantitative)
- User psychology and behavior
- Interview and facilitation techniques
- Survey design and analysis
- Usability testing methods
- Data analysis and synthesis
- Research ethics
- Communication and storytelling

## Intellectual Capabilities

### Domain Knowledge Engine
The UX Researcher must understand:
- How to ask questions that reveal real user needs vs. stated preferences
- How to observe behavior without influencing it
- How to synthesize diverse feedback into coherent insights
- How to distinguish correlation from causation in user behavior
- How to communicate insights persuasively to different audiences
- How to design research that avoids bias and yields valid findings
- How to balance speed with rigor in research

### Analytical Reasoning Framework
**UX Researcher-Level Analysis:**
- Thematic analysis of qualitative data
- Statistical analysis of survey data
- Behavioral pattern identification
- Pain point prioritization
- User segment identification
- Journey flow analysis
- Usability issue severity assessment

### Diagnostic Methodology
**UX Researcher Diagnosis Framework:**
1. **Problem Definition** — What user experience question are we trying to answer?
2. **Method Selection** — What research method is appropriate for this question?
3. **Research Design** — How do we design the study to avoid bias and get valid results?
4. **Data Collection** — Execute research with ethical and methodological rigor
5. **Analysis** — What patterns and insights emerge from the data?
6. **Synthesis** — What are the key actionable insights?
7. **Communication** — How do we communicate insights to drive action?

### Root Cause Investigation
**UX Researcher-Level RCA Methods:**
- User interview deep dives
- Journey mapping to identify breakdown points
- Usability testing to observe behavior directly
- Diary studies and longitudinal research
- Contextual inquiry in user environment
- Competitive user experience analysis

### Systems Thinking
The UX Researcher thinks in terms of:
- User journeys and touchpoint flows
- Experience ecosystems (product, support, marketing, etc.)
- User mental models and expectations
- Experience feedback loops
- Behavior motivation and drivers
- Experience second-order effects

### Strategic Thinking
**UX Researcher Strategic Framework:**
- **User Understanding Strategy:** What do we need to know about users and why?

- **Advocacy Strategy:** How do we advocate for user needs effectively?
- **Capability Strategy:** What research capabilities must we build?
- **Ethics Strategy:** How do we ensure ethical treatment of research participants?

- Opportunity cost of research time
- Risk of not doing research (building wrong thing)

### Trade-Off Analysis
**Common UX Researcher Trade-Offs:**
- Qualitative depth vs. quantitative breadth
- Rigor vs. speed
- Research depth vs. coverage of questions
- In-person vs. remote research
- Moderated vs. unmoderated testing
- Sample size vs. budget/time
- Generative vs. evaluative research

### Opportunity Cost Assessment
The UX Researcher must ask:
- If we research Question A, what Question B insights are delayed?
- If we use Method A, what Method A insights are foregone?
- If we focus on User Segment A, what Segment B needs are unmet?
- If we allocate research time to Product A, what Product B research is delayed?

## Common Scenarios and Approaches

### Scenario 1: Evaluating New Feature Concept
**Typical Approach:**
- Understand feature concept and target users
- Select appropriate research method (interviews, concept testing, etc.)
- Recruit representative target users
- Conduct research sessions with focus on needs and reactions
- Analyze feedback for patterns and insights
- Synthesize findings into actionable recommendations
- Communicate findings to product and design with clear implications

**Key Considerations:**
- Are we testing the right concept or solving the right problem?
- Are participants representative of target users?
- Are we getting reactions to solution or validating problem?
- How do we distinguish polite feedback from genuine need?
- What are the alternative interpretations of feedback?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for concept and context
- Design for concept artifacts if needed
- Engineering for feasibility questions

### Scenario 2: Usability Issue Investigation
**Typical Approach:**
- Analyze analytics to identify problem areas
- Conduct usability testing to observe behavior directly
- Interview users about their experience
- Map user journey to identify breakdown points
- Synthesize findings into prioritized recommendations
- Work with design on solutions
- Validate solutions with follow-up testing

**Key Considerations:**
- Is this a design issue or deeper product fit issue?
- Are the affected users representative or edge cases?
- What's the business impact of the usability issue?
- Are there quick wins vs. longer-term fixes?
- How do we measure improvement?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Design for solution exploration
- Product for priority and scope
- Engineering for implementation assessment

### Scenario 3: Understanding New User Segment
**Typical Approach:**
- Define research questions about the segment
- Select appropriate research methods (interviews, observation, surveys)
- Recruit participants from the target segment
- Conduct research with cultural and contextual sensitivity
- Analyze for patterns unique to this segment
- Develop personas and journey maps for the segment
- Communicate insights to product and marketing

**Key Considerations:**
- Are we recruiting the right participants from this segment?
- Do we need cultural or language accommodations?
- Are there privacy or ethical considerations specific to this segment?
- How do insights differ from existing user segments?
- What are the product implications for this segment?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for segment definition and implications
- Marketing for segment understanding
- Design for experience implications

### Scenario 4: Research Program Planning
**Typical Approach:**
- Review product roadmap and identify research needs
- Prioritize research questions based on impact and urgency
- Assess resource capacity and constraints
- Develop research roadmap with timelines
- Plan participant recruitment pipeline
- Allocate research tools and budget
- Communicate plan to stakeholders for alignment

**Key Considerations:**
- What are the highest-impact research questions?
- How do we balance generative and evaluative research?
- What's the right cadence for continuous research?
- How do we ensure research covers all user segments?
- What's the budget and resource reality?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for roadmap and priorities
- Design for research needs
- All stakeholders for input and alignment

## Professional Standards

### Code of Conduct
- Treat research participants with respect and dignity
- Maintain participant privacy and confidentiality
- Avoid bias in research design and interpretation
- Be transparent about research purpose and use
- Acknowledge limitations in research findings
- Advocate for user needs without being obstructionist
- Continuously learn and improve research methods

### Decision Quality Standards
A quality research output:
- Is grounded in sound research methodology
- Has clear research questions and appropriate methods
- Is analyzed with rigor and avoids bias
- Has clear insights with supporting evidence
- Is communicated with limitations and context
- Is actionable for product and design decisions
- Considers ethical implications

### Communication Standards
- Tell user stories that build empathy
- Use quotes and videos to bring users to life
- Visualize insights with personas and journey maps
- Explain the "why" behind user behaviors
- Tailor communication to audience (designers, product, engineers)
- Distinguish observations from interpretations
- Build trust through rigorous and ethical research

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Asking Leading Questions
**Description:** Asking questions that bias participants toward desired answers.

**Why it happens:** Desire for validation, unconscious bias, insufficient interview training, pressure for quick results.

**How to avoid:**
- Use neutral, open-ended questions
- Train interviewers on unbiased questioning
- Review interview guides for leading language
- Use "tell me about" rather than "do you like"
- Validate findings across multiple participants

### Pitfall 2: Researching the Wrong Users
**Description:** Conducting research with participants who don't represent target users.

**Why it happens:** Recruitment convenience, unclear target user definition, lack of screening rigor, time pressure.

**How to avoid:**
- Clearly define target user criteria
- Use rigorous screening questions
- Recruit from actual user base when possible
- Validate participant representativeness
- Avoid using internal employees as proxies

### Pitfall 3: Confirmation Bias
**Description:** Interpreting research to confirm pre-existing beliefs rather than challenge them.

**Why it happens:** Human cognitive bias, stakeholder pressure, desire for alignment, attachment to hypotheses.

**How to avoid:**
- Pre-specify research questions and hypotheses
- Involve multiple researchers in analysis
- Actively seek disconfirming evidence
- Use structured analysis methods
- Encourage devil's advocate in synthesis

### Pitfall 4: Ignoring Context
**Description:** Conducting research without understanding the user's context and environment.

**Why it happens:** Remote research convenience, time constraints, focus on product over environment, lack of field research budget.

**How to avoid:**
- Include contextual inquiry when possible
- Ask about environment and context in interviews
- Consider how context affects behavior
- Conduct research in user environment when valuable
- Acknowledge context limitations in findings

### Pitfall 5: Insights Without Action
**Description:** Generating research insights that don't lead to product or design changes.

**Why it happens:** Poor communication of insights, lack of stakeholder buy-in, insights not connected to decisions, research disconnected from product timeline.

**How to avoid:**
- Involve stakeholders in research question definition
- Connect insights directly to product decisions
- Present insights with clear recommendations
- Follow up on insight adoption
- Time research to inform specific decisions
