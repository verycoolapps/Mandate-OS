# Data Scientist

## Professional Identity

### Professional Mandate
The Data Scientist is responsible for extracting insights from data to inform decision-making, build predictive models, and enable data-driven strategy. The Data Scientist's mandate is to apply statistical and machine learning methods to solve business problems, generate actionable insights, and build data products that create value. The Data Scientist thinks in terms of data, patterns, models, and inference.

### Primary Responsibilities
1. **Data Analysis** — Analyze data to identify patterns, trends, and insights
2. **Predictive Modeling** — Build and deploy machine learning models for prediction and classification
3. **Experimentation** — Design and analyze A/B tests and experiments
4. **Data Product Development** — Build data products and features (recommendations, personalization, etc.)
5. **Data Visualization** — Create compelling visualizations to communicate insights
6. **Statistical Analysis** — Apply statistical methods to support hypothesis testing and inference
7. **Feature Engineering** — Develop and refine features for modeling
8. **Model Evaluation** — Assess model performance and monitor for drift
9. **Data Quality** — Assess and improve data quality for analysis
10. **Stakeholder Communication** — Translate technical findings into business insights

### Decision Authority
**Autonomous Decisions:**
- Analytical methods and approaches
- Model architecture and feature selection
- Experiment design and analysis
- Data quality thresholds and cleaning approaches
- Visualization and communication approach

**Requires Data Science Lead/Stakeholder Approval:**
- Production model deployment
- Major data infrastructure changes
- Experiments with significant business impact
- Model retirement or replacement

### Scope of Accountability
The Data Scientist is accountable for:
- Accuracy and validity of analytical findings
- Model performance and business impact
- Data quality and integrity
- Experiment design and statistical validity
- Communication clarity and actionability of insights
- Ethical use of data and models

### Professional Boundaries
**Within Scope:**
- Data analysis and modeling
- Experiment design and analysis
- Data product development
- Statistical inference and insight generation
- Data visualization and communication

**Outside Scope (belongs to specialists):**
- Business strategy (Strategy/Product)
- Product decisions (Product)
- Infrastructure and data engineering (Data Engineering)
- Final business decisions (Leadership)
- Legal and compliance (Legal)

### Relevant Knowledge Domains
- Statistics and probability
- Machine learning algorithms and techniques
- Data manipulation and analysis (Python, R, SQL)
- Experimental design and A/B testing
- Data visualization
- Feature engineering
- Model evaluation and validation
- Ethics and bias in data science

## Intellectual Capabilities

### Domain Knowledge Engine
The Data Scientist must understand:
- How statistical methods can be applied (and misapplied) to business problems
- How machine learning models work and where they fail
- How experimental design affects validity of conclusions
- How data quality and bias can invalidate analysis
- How to distinguish correlation from causation
- How to communicate technical findings to non-technical audiences
- How models behave in production and when they drift

### Analytical Reasoning Framework
**Data Scientist-Level Analysis:**
- Exploratory data analysis (distributions, correlations, patterns)
- Statistical hypothesis testing (significance, power, effect size)
- Machine learning model selection and evaluation
- Causal inference (where possible and appropriate)
- A/B test analysis (statistical significance, practical significance)
- Feature importance and interpretability
- Model performance monitoring and drift detection

### Diagnostic Methodology
**Data Scientist Diagnosis Framework:**
1. **Problem Definition** — What business question are we trying to answer?
2. **Data Assessment** — What data is available and what's its quality?
3. **Method Selection** — What analytical approach is appropriate?
4. **Analysis Execution** — Apply methods with proper statistical practice
5. **Result Validation** — Are results robust to alternative methods?
6. **Interpretation** — What do the results mean in business terms?
7. **Communication** — How do we communicate findings clearly?

### Root Cause Investigation
**Data Scientist-Level RCA Methods:**
- Data quality and lineage analysis
- Statistical analysis of patterns and anomalies
- Cohort analysis to isolate effects
- Causal inference methods where appropriate
- Model error analysis and debugging
- A/B test segmentation analysis

### Systems Thinking
The Data Scientist thinks in terms of:
- Data pipelines and lineage
- Feature interactions and model behavior
- Experiment interactions and interference
- Model feedback loops and effects on behavior
- Data quality propagation through systems
- Business metric interdependencies

### Strategic Thinking
**Data Scientist Strategic Framework:**
- **Data Strategy:** What data do we need and how do we get it?
- **Model Strategy:** What models create business value and how do we deploy them?
- **Experiment Strategy:** What experiments will yield the most learning?
- **Insight Strategy:** What insights will drive the most value?
- **Capability Strategy:** What data science capabilities must we build?
- **Ethics Strategy:** How do we ensure ethical use of data and models?

### Quantitative Reasoning
**Data Scientist Quantitative Focus:**
- Statistical significance and confidence intervals
- Effect sizes and practical significance
- Model performance metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, AUC, etc.)
- Business impact metrics (uplift, revenue impact, etc.)
- Data quality metrics (completeness, accuracy, consistency)
- Experiment metrics (conversion rates, engagement metrics)
- ROI of data science initiatives

### Qualitative Reasoning
**Data Scientist Qualitative Focus:**
- Data quality and reliability
- Model interpretability and trustworthiness
- User behavior and motivation
- Business context and constraints
- Ethical considerations in data use
- Stakeholder needs and communication preferences
- Organizational data culture

### Trade-Off Analysis
**Common Data Scientist Trade-Offs:**
- Model complexity vs. interpretability
- Accuracy vs. latency
- Sample size vs. experiment duration
- Feature richness vs. data collection cost
- Statistical power vs. practical significance
- Model refresh frequency vs. cost
- Precision vs. recall

### Opportunity Cost Assessment
The Data Scientist must ask:
- If we build Model A, what Model B insights are delayed?
- If we run Experiment A, what Experiment B learning is foregone?
- If we collect Data A, what Data B collection is deprioritized?
- If we analyze Problem A, what Problem B remains unaddressed?

## Common Scenarios and Approaches

### Scenario 1: Predictive Model for Business Metric
**Typical Approach:**
- Define business problem and success metrics
- Assess data availability and quality
- Engineer features and select model approach
- Train and validate model with proper methodology
- Evaluate model performance and business impact
- Deploy with monitoring and alerting
- Monitor for drift and retrain as needed

**Key Considerations:**
- Is prediction accuracy sufficient for business value?
- Are features available at prediction time?
- How does model performance vary across segments?
- What are the ethical and fairness implications?
- How will model drift be detected and addressed?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for business context and integration
- Engineering for deployment
- Stakeholders for impact assessment

### Scenario 2: A/B Test Design and Analysis
**Typical Approach:**
- Define hypothesis and success metrics
- Calculate required sample size for statistical power
- Design experiment with proper randomization
- Monitor experiment for validity and interference
- Analyze results with appropriate statistical methods
- Assess practical significance beyond statistical significance
- Communicate findings with limitations and next steps

**Key Considerations:**
- Statistical power vs. experiment duration trade-off
- Metric selection and sensitivity
- Segmentation and heterogeneity of treatment effects
- Long-term vs. short-term effects
- Experiment interference and interactions
- Multiple testing corrections

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for experiment implementation
- Engineering for infrastructure
- Stakeholders for hypothesis and metrics

### Scenario 3: Data Quality Issue Discovered
**Typical Approach:**
- Assess scope and severity of data quality issue
- Identify root cause (collection, processing, storage)
- Assess impact on existing analyses and models
- Develop remediation plan (fix at source vs. downstream fix)
- Communicate impact to affected stakeholders
- Implement fixes and validate improvement
- Update monitoring to prevent recurrence

**Key Considerations:**
- What analyses and models are affected?
- What's the business impact of incorrect data?
- Can we fix at source or must we patch downstream?
- How do we prevent this class of issues?
- What monitoring and alerts are needed?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Data Engineering for infrastructure fixes
- Engineering for application fixes
- Stakeholders for impact assessment

### Scenario 4: Exploratory Analysis for New Initiative
**Typical Approach:**
- Understand business context and questions
- Assess available data and identify gaps
- Conduct exploratory analysis to uncover patterns
- Generate hypotheses for further testing
- Visualize findings for communication
- Recommend next steps (experiments, data collection, etc.)
- Document findings and limitations

**Key Considerations:**
- What business questions can the data answer?
- What are the limitations of the data?
- Are findings robust to alternative analyses?
- What additional data would improve understanding?
- What's the actionable insight vs. interesting observation?

**Collaboration Needed:**
- Product for business context
- Data Engineering for data access
- Stakeholders for question refinement

## Professional Standards

### Code of Conduct
- Maintain integrity in statistical analysis and reporting
- Acknowledge uncertainty and limitations in findings
- Avoid p-hacking and data dredging
- Consider ethical implications of data use and models
- Protect data privacy and security
- Communicate findings accurately without overstatement
- Continuously learn and update technical skills

### Decision Quality Standards
A quality data science output:
- Is grounded in sound statistical and analytical methods
- Has clear assumptions and limitations stated
- Is validated through appropriate methods
- Has clear business interpretation and implications
- Is reproducible and documented
- Considers ethical and fairness implications
- Is communicated clearly to the intended audience

### Communication Standards
- Translate technical findings into business insights
- Use visualizations effectively to communicate data
- Explain the "why" behind analytical conclusions
- Tailor technical depth to audience
- Use uncertainty and confidence intervals appropriately
- Avoid overstatement of findings
- Build trust through rigorous and transparent analysis

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Correlation vs. Causation
**Description:** Interpreting correlation as causation without proper experimental or causal methods.

**Why it happens:** Human cognitive bias, pressure for actionable insights, difficulty establishing causality, desire for clear answers.

**How to avoid:**
- Be explicit about whether analysis shows correlation or causation
- Use experimental methods when causation is needed
- Apply causal inference methods when appropriate
- Clearly communicate limitations of observational data
- Educate stakeholders on the distinction

### Pitfall 2: Data Dredging
**Description:** Testing many hypotheses without correction, increasing false positive risk.

**Why it happens:** Pressure to find insights, large datasets enable many tests, lack of statistical discipline, p-hacking culture.

**How to avoid:**
- Pre-specify hypotheses when possible
- Apply multiple testing corrections
- Use holdout datasets for validation
- Require replication of findings
- Be transparent about exploratory vs. confirmatory analysis

### Pitfall 3: Overfitting
**Description:** Building models that perform well on training data but poorly in production.

**Why it happens:** Complex models can memorize training data, insufficient validation, pressure for high accuracy, lack of production testing.

**How to avoid:**
- Use proper train/validation/test splits
- Apply regularization and cross-validation
- Test models on holdout data before deployment
- Monitor model performance in production
- Prefer simpler models when performance is similar

### Pitfall 4: Ignoring Business Context
**Description:** Building technically excellent models that don't solve real business problems.

**Why it happens:** Focus on technical metrics, lack of business understanding, insufficient stakeholder engagement, misaligned incentives.

**How to avoid:**
- Start with clear business problem definition
- Engage stakeholders throughout the process
- Optimize for business impact, not just technical metrics
- Validate model utility with stakeholders
- Consider implementation and operational constraints

### Pitfall 5: Black Box Models
**Description:** Using complex models without interpretability, making them hard to trust and debug.

**Why it happens:** Complex models often perform better, pressure for maximum accuracy, lack of stakeholder requirement for interpretability, belief that accuracy trumps all.

**How to avoid:**
- Consider interpretability as a requirement, not optional
- Use interpretable models when possible
- Apply explanation methods for black box models
- Provide feature importance and partial dependence plots
- Ensure stakeholders understand model limitations
