# Impact Analysis Framework

## PURPOSE

The Impact Analysis Framework systematically evaluates the consequences of decisions across multiple dimensions. It ensures that decisions are made with full understanding of their effects—intended and unintended, immediate and long-term.

---

## CORE PHILOSOPHY

**Every action has consequences. Professional decisions require understanding the full ripple effect.**

Impact analysis prevents:
- Unintended negative consequences
- Underestimation of downstream effects
- Failure to consider second and third-order effects
- Ignoring stakeholder impacts
- Overlooking long-term implications

---

## IMPACT ANALYSIS PROTOCOL

### Step 1: Scope the Analysis
**What is being analyzed?**

Define:
- Decision or change being evaluated
- Analysis boundaries (what's in scope)
- Timeframe for analysis
- Stakeholders to consider
- Impact dimensions relevant to the decision

### Step 2: Identify First-Order Effects
**Direct, immediate consequences**

For each impact dimension:
- What directly changes?
- How much does it change?
- When does the change occur?
- Who is affected?
- Is the effect positive or negative?

**Documentation Format:**
```
Dimension: [Dimension Name]
Effect: [Description of direct effect]
Magnitude: [High/Medium/Low or quantitative]
Direction: [Positive/Negative/Mixed]
Timing: [Immediate/Short-term/Medium-term/Long-term]
Affected Parties: [Who is affected]
```

### Step 3: Identify Second-Order Effects
**Consequences of the consequences**

For each first-order effect:
- What does this effect cause?
- What chains of reaction occur?
- Where do effects compound?
- Where do effects offset?

**Example Chain:**
- **First-Order:** Reduce headcount by 10%
- **Second-Order:** Remaining employees have increased workload
- **Second-Order:** Employee satisfaction decreases
- **Second-Order:** Turnover risk increases
- **Second-Order:** Institutional knowledge loss accelerates

### Step 4: Identify Third-Order Effects
**Systemic, long-term impacts**

For patterns in second-order effects:
- What systemic changes occur?
- What cultural shifts emerge?
- What competitive position changes?
- What market dynamics shift?

**Example:**
- **Second-Order Pattern:** Continuous cost-cutting cycles
- **Third-Order:** Culture of risk aversion and underinvestment
- **Third-Order:** Loss of innovation capability
- **Third-Order:** Long-term competitive decline

### Step 5: Quantify Impacts Where Possible
**Convert qualitative to quantitative**

**Quantification Approaches:**
- **Financial:** Revenue, cost, margin, cash flow impacts in dollars
- **Operational:** Efficiency gains in % or time saved
- **Customer:** Churn rate, NPS, CLV changes
- **Employee:** Turnover rate, engagement score changes
- **Time:** Days/weeks/months to effect

**When Quantification Not Possible:**
- Use structured qualitative assessment (High/Medium/Low)
- Use comparative assessment (Better/Worse/Same)
- Use scenario ranges (Best case/Base case/Worst case)

### Step 6: Assess Impact Magnitude
**How significant are the effects?**

**Magnitude Assessment:**

| Magnitude | Definition | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| Critical | Existential or severe impact | Regulatory violation, major revenue loss |
| High | Significant impact requiring action | 10%+ revenue change, major process disruption |
| Medium | Moderate impact manageable with effort | 5-10% revenue change, process adjustment needed |
| Low | Minor impact easily managed | <5% revenue change, minor process tweak |
| Negligible | Minimal impact not requiring action | <1% revenue change, no process change |

### Step 7: Assess Impact Direction
**Positive, negative, or mixed?**

**Direction Classification:**
- **Positive:** Net beneficial effect
- **Negative:** Net detrimental effect
- **Mixed:** Both positive and negative effects
- **Neutral:** No significant effect

**For mixed effects:**
- Separate positive and negative components
- Calculate net effect where possible
- Identify who wins and who loses

### Step 8: Identify Impact Mitigation
**How can negative impacts be reduced?**

For each negative impact:
- Can it be prevented?
- Can it be reduced?
- Can it be transferred?
- Can it be accepted with monitoring?

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- **Prevention:** Eliminate the cause
- **Reduction:** Lessen the severity
- **Transfer:** Shift to another party (insurance, contract)
- **Acceptance:** Monitor and manage

### Step 9: Document Impact Summary
**Clear summary of all impacts**

**Impact Summary Template:**

```
CRITICAL IMPACTS:
- [Impact 1]: [Description] - [Magnitude] - [Direction] - [Mitigation]

HIGH IMPACTS:
- [Impact 1]: [Description] - [Magnitude] - [Direction] - [Mitigation]

MEDIUM IMPACTS:
- [Impact 1]: [Description] - [Magnitude] - [Direction] - [Mitigation]

LOW IMPACTS:
- [Impact 1]: [Description] - [Magnitude] - [Direction] - [Mitigation]

NET ASSESSMENT:
- Overall impact: [Positive/Negative/Mixed]
- Primary beneficiaries: [Who gains]
- Primary affected parties: [Who loses]
- Most significant risk: [What could go wrong]
- Recommended mitigation: [What to do]
```

## COMMON IMPACT ANALYSIS SCENARIOS

### Scenario 1: Product Launch
**First-Order:**
- Revenue increase from new product
- Development cost incurred
- Marketing spend required
- Sales team training needed

**Second-Order:**
- Existing product cannibalization
- Support burden increase
- Competitive response
- Customer confusion if positioning unclear

**Third-Order:**
- Brand positioning shift
- Market category redefinition
- Platform for future products
- Talent attraction (innovative brand)

### Scenario 2: Pricing Change
**First-Order:**
- Revenue per unit change
- Volume change (demand elasticity)
- Customer satisfaction impact
- Competitive position change

**Second-Order:**
- Customer segment shifts
- Churn impact
- Sales compensation impact
- Channel partner margins

**Third-Order:**
- Market position redefinition
- Brand perception (premium/value)
- Competitive pricing response
- Long-term customer relationship impact

### Scenario 3: Organizational Restructure
**First-Order:**
- Reporting structure changes
- Role changes
- Potential headcount reduction
- Communication changes

**Second-Order:**
- Decision speed changes
- Collaboration patterns change
- Culture impact
- Morale impact

**Third-Order:**
- Organizational agility
- Innovation capability
- Talent attraction/retention
- Institutional knowledge retention

### Scenario 4: Technology Migration
**First-Order:**
- Migration cost
- Temporary disruption
- New capabilities
- Learning curve

**Second-Order:**
- Process efficiency changes
- Skill requirements change
- Vendor dependency
- Technical debt reduction/increase

**Third-Order:**
- Technology platform modernization
- Innovation capability
- Scalability improvements
- Long-term cost structure

## IMPACT ANALYSIS PITFALLS

### Common Mistakes

**1. Focusing Only on First-Order Effects**
- Ignoring downstream consequences
- Missing compounding effects
- Underestimating long-term impacts

**2. Optimism Bias**
- Overestimating positive impacts
- Underestimating negative impacts
- Ignoring implementation challenges

**3. Narrow Scope**
- Focusing only on financial impacts
- Ignoring stakeholder impacts
- Missing cross-functional effects

**4. Static Analysis**
- Not considering dynamic responses
- Ignoring competitive reactions
- Missing market feedback loops

**5. Linear Thinking**
- Assuming simple cause-effect
- Missing complex interactions
- Ignoring systemic effects

## IMPACT ANALYSIS IN DECISION MAKING

### When to Use Comprehensive Impact Analysis
- Strategic decisions
- Major investments
- Organizational changes
- Product launches
- Market entries
- Technology platform changes
- Mergers and acquisitions

### When to Use Simplified Impact Analysis
- Operational decisions
- Tactical choices
- Low-risk changes
- Routine improvements
- Minor adjustments

### Impact Analysis Triggers
- Decision magnitude exceeds threshold
- Stakeholder impact is significant
- Financial impact exceeds threshold
- Risk profile changes significantly
- Regulatory implications exist
- Customer impact is material

## STANDARDS

**IMPACT ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK**
- Every significant decision includes impact analysis
- First, second, and third-order effects are considered
- Multiple impact dimensions are evaluated
- Positive and negative impacts are documented
- Magnitude and direction are assessed
- Mitigation strategies are identified for negative impacts
- Stakeholder impacts are explicitly considered
- Quantification is used where possible
- Qualitative assessment is structured where quantification is impossible
