# Risk Intelligence Framework

## PURPOSE

The Risk Intelligence Framework provides systematic methods for identifying, evaluating, and managing risks. It ensures that decisions are made with full understanding of what could go wrong and how to respond.

---

## CORE PHILOSOPHY

**Every decision carries risk. Professional management requires explicit risk assessment, not blind optimism.**

Risk intelligence prevents:
- Surprise failures
- Unprepared crisis responses
- Hidden vulnerabilities
- Concentrated exposures
- Systemic blind spots

---

## RISK TAXONOMY

### 1. Financial Risk
**Money-related risks**

**Types:**
- **Revenue Risk:** Revenue below projections
- **Cost Risk:** Costs exceeding budget
- **Cash Flow Risk:** Liquidity constraints
- **Credit Risk:** Counterparty default
- **Market Risk:** Market value fluctuations
- **Currency Risk:** Exchange rate volatility
- **Interest Rate Risk:** Rate changes affecting costs/investments

### 2. Operational Risk
**Day-to-day execution risks**

**Types:**
- **Process Risk:** Process failures or inefficiencies
- **People Risk:** Key person dependency, skill gaps
- **System Risk:** IT system failures
- **Supply Chain Risk:** Supplier failures, disruptions
- **Quality Risk:** Quality failures, defects
- **Capacity Risk:** Insufficient capacity
- **Execution Risk:** Implementation failures

### 3. Strategic Risk
**Long-term positioning risks**

**Types:**
- **Market Risk:** Market changes, shifts
- **Competitive Risk:** Competitive actions, new entrants
- **Technology Risk:** Technology disruption, obsolescence
- **Innovation Risk:** Failure to innovate
- **Strategic Misalignment:** Strategy not aligned with market
- **M&A Risk:** Integration failures, overpayment
- **Brand Risk:** Brand damage, reputation loss

### 4. Regulatory/Legal Risk
**Compliance and legal risks**

**Types:**
- **Compliance Risk:** Regulatory violations
- **Legal Risk:** Lawsuits, litigation
- **Contract Risk:** Contract breaches, disputes
- **Regulatory Change Risk:** New regulations
- **Intellectual Property Risk:** IP infringement, loss
- **Data Privacy Risk:** Privacy violations
- **Employment Risk:** Labor law violations

### 5. Reputational Risk
**Reputation and trust risks**

**Types:**
- **Customer Trust Risk:** Loss of customer confidence
- **Brand Damage Risk:** Negative publicity
- **Social Media Risk:** Viral negative sentiment
- **Stakeholder Trust Risk:** Loss of investor/partner trust
- **Ethics Risk:** Unethical behavior exposure

### 6. Technology Risk
**Technical and cybersecurity risks**

**Types:**
- **Security Risk:** Data breaches, cyberattacks
- **Data Risk:** Data loss, corruption
- **Technology Obsolescence Risk:** Technology becoming outdated
- **Integration Risk:** System integration failures
- **Scalability Risk:** Systems not scaling
- **Vendor Lock-In Risk:** Dependency on specific vendors
- **Technical Debt Risk:** Accumulated technical debt

### 7. Human Capital Risk
**Workforce-related risks**

**Types:**
- **Talent Risk:** Inability to attract/retain talent
- **Key Person Risk:** Dependency on specific individuals
- **Skill Gap Risk:** Missing critical skills
- **Culture Risk:** Toxic culture, disengagement
- **Succession Risk:** No succession planning
- **Turnover Risk:** Excessive turnover

### 8. External Risk
**Outside organization risks**

**Types:**
- **Macroeconomic Risk:** Economic downturns, recessions
- **Geopolitical Risk:** Political instability, trade conflicts
- **Natural Disaster Risk:** Natural events disrupting operations
- **Pandemic Risk:** Health crises
- **Infrastructure Risk:** Critical infrastructure failures

## RISK ASSESSMENT PROTOCOL

### Step 1: Risk Identification
**What could go wrong?**

**Identification Methods:**
- **Brainstorming:** Structured brainstorming with diverse perspectives
- **Checklists:** Domain-specific risk checklists
- **Historical Analysis:** Review of past failures
- **Scenario Analysis:** "What if" scenarios
- **Expert Consultation:** Domain expert input
- **Benchmarking:** Industry risk profiles
- **Red Teaming:** Deliberate challenge of assumptions

**For each identified risk:**
- Risk description
- Risk category
- Risk owner (who is responsible)
- Risk trigger (what causes it)

### Step 2: Risk Likelihood Assessment
**How likely is this risk?**

The likelihood bands below are heuristic prompts for consistent prioritization, not measured probabilities or calibrated estimates. Use them as qualitative labels unless relevant data supports a numerical estimate for the specific population, exposure period, and context. Never present a heuristic band or unsupported expert judgment as a measured probability.

**Illustrative Heuristic Likelihood Bands:**

| Level | Heuristic band | Description | Example |
|-------|----------------|-------------|---------|
| Almost Certain | >90% | Expected to occur in the stated context | Known regulatory change |
| Likely | 70-90% | More likely than not | Market downturn in recession |
| Possible | 30-70% | Plausible, with material uncertainty | Competitor launching similar product |
| Unlikely | 10-30% | Possible but not expected | Key person leaving unexpectedly |
| Rare | <10% | Uncommon, but still possible | Natural disaster disrupting HQ |

Use the band as a heuristic only unless the estimate is grounded in relevant data. If that basis is unavailable, report the qualitative likelihood label and explain its rationale.

**Assessment Methods:**
- **Relevant Historical Data:** Use rates only when population, exposure period, and context are comparable.
- **Expert Judgment:** Record as a subjective estimate or qualitative assessment, not a measured probability.
- **Statistical Models:** Report numerical probabilities only where the model is validated for the context and its assumptions are stated.
- **Industry Benchmarks:** Treat as directional unless applicability to this context is established.

### Step 3: Risk Impact Assessment
**How severe would it be if it occurred?**

**Impact Scale:**

| Level | Impact | Description | Example |
|-------|--------|-------------|---------|
| Catastrophic | Existential threat | Organizational failure or prolonged loss of critical capability | Bankruptcy, regulatory shutdown |
| Critical | Severe impact | Major damage or extended recovery; exceeds critical thresholds set by organizational policy | Major lawsuit or material financial loss |
| Major | Significant impact | Material damage requiring substantial recovery; assess against context-specific tolerances | Material revenue or service disruption, major PR crisis |
| Moderate | Manageable impact | Recoverable with planned effort and limited lasting harm | Localized process disruption or temporary financial loss |
| Minor | Minimal impact | Easily recoverable with negligible lasting harm | Minor delay or localized service issue |

Set quantitative thresholds using organization policy and the relevant business context. These qualitative descriptions are not universal financial benchmarks.

**Impact Dimensions:**
- Financial impact
- Operational impact
- Strategic impact
- Reputational impact
- Legal/regulatory impact
- Human impact

### Step 4: Risk Prioritization
**Which risks need the most attention?**

**Risk Matrix (heuristic likelihood × impact):**

This matrix yields prioritization categories, not measured risk probabilities. Apply the qualitative likelihood labels above unless relevant data supports and clearly qualifies a numerical estimate.

| | Catastrophic | Critical | Major | Moderate | Minor |
|---|--------------|----------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Almost Certain** | Extreme | Extreme | High | High | Medium |
| **Likely** | Extreme | High | High | Medium | Low |
| **Possible** | High | High | Medium | Low | Low |
| **Unlikely** | High | Medium | Low | Low | Very Low |
| **Rare** | Medium | Low | Low | Very Low | Very Low |

**Priority Levels:**
- **Extreme:** Immediate action required
- **High:** Action required within defined timeframe
- **Medium:** Monitor and plan response
- **Low:** Monitor periodically
- **Very Low:** Accept and document

### Step 5: Risk Response Planning
**How will we respond if the risk occurs?**

**Response Strategies:**

**Avoid:**
- Eliminate the risk by changing plans
- Not undertake the risky activity
- Example: Don't enter high-risk market

**Reduce (Mitigate):**
- Reduce probability of occurrence
- Reduce impact if it occurs
- Example: Implement controls, diversify

**Transfer:**
- Transfer risk to another party
- Insurance, contracts, warranties
- Example: Purchase cyber insurance

**Accept:**
- Accept risk and prepare contingency
- Monitor and respond if needed
- Example: Accept market volatility, have contingency plan

**For each risk:**
- Primary response strategy
- Specific mitigation actions
- Contingency plan if risk occurs
- Owner for implementation
- Timeline for implementation

### Step 6: Risk Monitoring
**How will we track the risk?**

**Monitoring Mechanisms:**
- **Leading Indicators:** Early warning signs
- **Lagging Indicators:** After-the-fact measures
- **Trigger Points:** Thresholds that trigger action
- **Regular Reviews:** Scheduled risk assessment updates
- **Event-Driven Reviews:** Reassessment after major changes

**Monitoring Frequency:**
- **Extreme:** Continuous or daily
- **High:** Weekly
- **Medium:** Monthly
- **Low:** Quarterly
- **Very Low:** Annually

## RISK REGISTER TEMPLATE

### Standard Risk Register Format

| Risk ID | Risk Description | Category | Probability | Impact | Risk Level | Owner | Response Strategy | Mitigation Actions | Status | Last Review |
|---------|------------------|----------|-------------|--------|------------|-------|------------------|-------------------|--------|-------------|
| R001 | [Description] | [Category] | [Level] | [Level] | [Priority] | [Name] | [Strategy] | [Actions] | [Open/Mitigated/Closed] | [Date] |
| R002 | [Description] | [Category] | [Level] | [Level] | [Priority] | [Name] | [Strategy] | [Actions] | [Open/Mitigated/Closed] | [Date] |

### Enhanced Risk Register (optional quantitative fields)

Use numerical probability only when supported by relevant data or a stated, fit-for-purpose model; identify whether it is an observed rate or an estimate and give its basis. Otherwise record the qualitative likelihood label. A score calculated from heuristic inputs is a prioritization aid, not a measured probability or calibrated risk measure.

| Risk ID | Risk Description | Category | Probability or likelihood basis | Financial Impact ($) | Non-Financial Impact | Risk Score | Owner | Response Strategy | Mitigation Actions | Cost of Mitigation | Residual Risk | Status |
|---------|------------------|----------|---------------------------------|----------------------|---------------------|------------|-------|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|--------|
| R001 | [Description] | [Category] | [Qualitative label or data-based estimate + basis] | [Amount] | [Description] | [Heuristic calculation] | [Name] | [Strategy] | [Actions] | [Amount] | [Score] | [Status] |

**Illustrative Risk Score Calculation:**
```
Risk Score = Probability (%) × Impact (1-5 scale)
Impact Scale: Catastrophic=5, Critical=4, Major=3, Moderate=2, Minor=1
```
The formula is a prioritization rubric, not a calibrated measure. A heuristic probability input yields a heuristic score; do not describe it as a measured probability.

## COMMON RISK SCENARIOS

### Scenario 1: Product Launch Risk
**Identified Risks:**
- **Market Acceptance Risk:** Customers don't want the product (Possible, Major)
- **Technical Risk:** Product has bugs/defects (Possible, Major)
- **Competitive Response Risk:** Competitors launch better product (Unlikely, Critical)
- **Timing Risk:** Launch delayed (Possible, Moderate)
- **Pricing Risk:** Price too high/low (Possible, Moderate)

**Response Strategy:**
- Market research before launch (Reduce probability)
- Beta testing to catch bugs (Reduce probability)
- Competitive monitoring (Monitor)
- Contingency pricing (Reduce impact)
- Phased launch (Reduce impact)

### Scenario 2: Technology Migration Risk
**Identified Risks:**
- **Data Loss Risk:** Data corrupted during migration (Unlikely, Catastrophic)
- **Downtime Risk:** System unavailable during migration (Possible, Major)
- **Integration Risk:** Systems don't integrate properly (Possible, Critical)
- **Cost Overrun Risk:** Migration exceeds budget (Likely, Major)
- **Skill Gap Risk:** Team lacks new technology skills (Possible, Moderate)

**Response Strategy:**
- Comprehensive backup before migration (Reduce impact)
- Phased migration with rollback (Reduce impact)
- Extensive testing (Reduce probability)
- Budget contingency (Reduce impact)
- Training program (Reduce probability)

### Scenario 3: Market Expansion Risk
**Identified Risks:**
- **Market Understanding Risk:** Don't understand local market (Possible, Major)
- **Regulatory Risk:** Local regulations block entry (Unlikely, Critical)
- **Cultural Risk:** Product doesn't fit local culture (Possible, Major)
- **Partner Risk:** Local partner fails (Possible, Major)
- **Currency Risk:** Exchange rate volatility (Likely, Moderate)

**Response Strategy:**
- Local market research (Reduce probability)
- Legal review (Reduce probability)
- Localization and adaptation (Reduce probability)
- Partner due diligence (Reduce probability)
- Currency hedging (Transfer risk)

## RISK AGGREGATION AND PORTFOLIO VIEW

### Risk Concentration
**Are risks concentrated in specific areas?**

**Concentration Analysis:**
- By category (too many financial risks?)
- By business unit (concentration in one area?)
- By geography (concentration in one region?)
- By supplier (dependency on single supplier?)
- By customer (dependency on few large customers?)

### Risk Correlation
**Do risks move together?**

**Correlation Analysis:**
- **Positive Correlation:** Risks that occur together (e.g., economic downturn + revenue decline)
- **Negative Correlation:** Risks that offset each other (rare)
- **Independent Risks:** Unrelated risks

**Implications:**
- Highly correlated risks create concentration risk
- Diversified risk portfolio is more resilient

### Risk Capacity
**How much risk can the organization absorb?**

**Capacity Dimensions:**
- **Financial Capacity:** Can absorb financial losses
- **Operational Capacity:** Can absorb operational disruptions
- **Reputational Capacity:** Can absorb reputation damage
- **Strategic Capacity:** Can absorb strategic setbacks

**For Executives:**

- Risk mitigation progress

- Enterprise risk profile

- Risk appetite alignment
- Insurance coverage
- Regulatory compliance

- Operational risks specific to their area
- Mitigation actions required
- Monitoring responsibilities
- Contingency procedures

**For Investors:**
- Material risks
- Risk management approach
- Historical risk events
- Risk mitigation effectiveness

### Risk Communication Principles

**Be Transparent:**
- Don't hide risks
- Acknowledge uncertainty
- Distinguish known vs. unknown risks

**Be Clear:**
- Use plain language
- Avoid jargon
- Provide context

**Be Action-Oriented:**
- Focus on what is being done
- Provide mitigation plans
- Define accountabilities

## RISK CULTURE

### Building Risk Intelligence Culture

**Leadership:**
- Leaders acknowledge risks openly
- Leaders reward risk identification
- Leaders don't punish bad news
- Leaders model risk-aware decision making

**Processes:**
- Risk assessment is standard in decisions
- Risk register is maintained and used
- Risk reviews are scheduled and acted on
- Lessons learned are captured

**Incentives:**
- Risk identification is rewarded
- Responsible risk-taking is encouraged
- Hidden risks are discovered and addressed
- Risk mitigation is recognized

## RISK INTELLIGENCE PITFALLS

### Common Mistakes

**1. Optimism Bias**
- Underestimating probability
- Underestimating impact
- Overestimating mitigation effectiveness

**2. Hindsight Bias**
- "We should have seen it coming" (in reality, it wasn't obvious)
- Overreacting to past failures
- Focusing on recent risks at expense of others

**3. Confirmation Bias**
- Seeking evidence that risks are low
- Ignoring warning signs
- Dismissing contrary opinions

**4. Normalization of Deviance**
- Accepting small risks as normal
- Gradually increasing risk tolerance
- Ignoring accumulating warning signs

**5. Sunk Cost Fallacy**
- Continuing risky projects because of investment
- Not cutting losses when risks materialize
- Escalating commitment to failing courses

## STANDARDS

**RISK INTELLIGENCE FRAMEWORK**
- Every significant decision includes risk assessment
- Risks are identified, assessed, and prioritized
- Risk register is maintained and updated
- Response strategies are defined for priority risks
- Risk monitoring is ongoing
- Risk communication is transparent and stakeholder-specific
- Risk culture encourages open identification of risks
- Risk aggregation and concentration are monitored
- Risk capacity is understood and respected
