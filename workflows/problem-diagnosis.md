# PROBLEM DIAGNOSIS WORKFLOW

**MANDATE 360° Professional Analysis Protocol — Mode: DIAGNOSIS**

---

## PURPOSE

Systematically investigate and understand the true nature of a problem before proposing solutions. This workflow prevents solution-first thinking by requiring complete problem understanding, root cause identification, and evidence gathering.

---

## DIAGNOSIS PROTOCOL

### Phase 1: Problem Definition

#### 1.1 Surface Problem Statement
- What is the observable issue?
- When was it first noticed?
- Who is affected?
- What are the visible symptoms?

#### 1.2 Problem Boundary
- What is clearly within scope?
- What is outside scope?
- What is unclear or ambiguous?

#### 1.3 Stakeholder Identification
- Who experiences the problem?
- Who is responsible for addressing it?
- Who has decision authority?
- Who provides necessary information?

#### 1.4 Impact Quantification
- Magnitude: How severe is the problem?
- Urgency: Timeline for action
- Scale: Number of affected parties
- Frequency: One-time or recurring

---

### Phase 2: Evidence Gathering

#### 2.1 Observable Facts
What can be measured, observed, or verified:
- Quantitative data points
- Observable behaviors
- System outputs
- Documented incidents

#### 2.2 Contextual Information
- Recent changes in the system
- Historical patterns
- Environmental factors
- External influences

#### 2.3 Stakeholder Perspectives
- Different descriptions of the problem
- Conflicting interpretations
- Hidden agendas or incentives
- Information asymmetries

#### 2.4 Evidence Quality Assessment
For each piece of evidence:
- Source reliability
- Measurement accuracy
- Recency
- Completeness
- Potential bias

---

### Phase 3: Root Cause Investigation

#### 3.1 Five Whys Analysis
Ask "Why?" repeatedly until reaching fundamental cause:
1. Why is the problem occurring?
2. Why is that cause present?
3. Why did that condition develop?
4. Why was that allowed to persist?
5. Why was the underlying system designed that way?

#### 3.2 Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa)
Categorize potential causes:
- **People:** Training, staffing, communication
- **Process:** Procedures, workflows, protocols
- **Equipment:** Tools, infrastructure, technology
- **Materials:** Resources, inputs, data
- **Environment:** Conditions, culture, external factors
- **Management:** Leadership, oversight, governance

#### 3.3 System Mapping
- Diagram the relevant system components
- Identify feedback loops
- Locate bottlenecks and constraints
- Map interdependencies

#### 3.4 Temporal Analysis
- When did the problem start?
- What changed around that time?
- Is the problem worsening or stable?
- Are there cyclical patterns?

---

### Phase 4: Hypothesis Generation

#### 4.1 Working Hypotheses
Generate multiple plausible explanations:
- Hypothesis A: [Description] — [Supporting evidence] — [Contradicting evidence]
- Hypothesis B: [Description] — [Supporting evidence] — [Contradicting evidence]
- Hypothesis C: [Description] — [Supporting evidence] — [Contradicting evidence]

#### 4.2 Hypothesis Evaluation
For each hypothesis:
- Explanatory power: How much does it explain?
- Consistency: Does it fit all evidence?
- Testability: Can it be verified?
- Plausibility: Is it realistically possible?
- Simplicity: Is it unnecessarily complex?

#### 4.3 Confidence Levels
- **High Confidence:** Strong evidence, minimal contradiction
- **Medium Confidence:** Partial evidence, some uncertainty
- **Low Confidence:** Limited evidence, significant uncertainty
- **Unknown:** Insufficient information to assess

---

### Phase 5: Diagnostic Testing

#### 5.1 Test Design
What evidence would confirm or refute each hypothesis?
- Specific measurements to take
- Experiments to run
- Questions to ask
- Data to collect

#### 5.2 Test Execution
- Conduct diagnostic tests
- Document methodology
- Record results objectively
- Maintain control conditions

#### 5.3 Result Interpretation
- Does evidence support hypothesis?
- Are results consistent?
- What new questions emerge?
- Are additional tests needed?

---

### Phase 6: Problem Classification

#### 6.1 Problem Type
- **Symptom vs. Root Cause:** Are we treating symptoms or addressing causes?
- **Single vs. Systemic:** Is this isolated or indicative of broader issues?
- **Technical vs. Human:** Is the cause in systems or people/processes?
- **External vs. Internal:** Is the source outside or within control?

#### 6.2 Complexity Assessment
- **Simple:** Clear cause, straightforward solution
- **Complicated:** Multiple causes, requires expertise
- **Complex:** Emergent behavior, unpredictable outcomes
- **Chaotic:** No clear cause-and-effect relationship

#### 6.3 Solvability Assessment
- **Addressable:** Within authority and resources
- **Partially Addressable:** Can mitigate but not eliminate
- **Requires Escalation:** Beyond current authority
- **Intractable:** Not solvable with current constraints

---

### Phase 7: Diagnostic Report

#### 7.1 Executive Summary
- What is the problem?
- What is the root cause?
- What is the confidence level?
- What should be done next?

#### 7.2 Detailed Findings
- Evidence summary
- Hypothesis evaluation
- Test results
- Reasoning chain

#### 7.3 Uncertainties and Gaps
- What remains unknown?
- What information is missing?
- What assumptions were made?
- What requires further investigation?

#### 7.4 Recommended Next Steps
- Immediate actions
- Additional investigation needed
- Stakeholder communications
- Decision points

---

## QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before concluding diagnosis:

- [ ] Problem is clearly defined
- [ ] Evidence is documented and sourced
- [ ] Multiple hypotheses were considered
- [ ] Root cause investigation went beyond surface symptoms
- [ ] System interdependencies were examined
- [ ] Confidence levels are stated explicitly
- [ ] Uncertainties are acknowledged
- [ ] Assumptions are identified
- [ ] Recommendations are evidence-based
- [ ] No premature solutions were proposed

---

## COMMON DIAGNOSTIC ERRORS TO AVOID

### Solution Jumping
- **Error:** Proposing solutions before understanding the problem
- **Prevention:** Complete all diagnostic phases before solution discussion

### Confirmation Bias
- **Error:** Seeking evidence that confirms pre-existing beliefs
- **Prevention:** Actively search for contradictory evidence

### Symptom Treatment
- **Error:** Addressing visible symptoms while ignoring root causes
- **Prevention:** Use Five Whys to trace to fundamental causes

### Single-Cause Fallacy
- **Error:** Assuming problems have single, simple causes
- **Prevention:** Consider multiple, interacting causes

### Attribution Error
- **Error:** Blaming individuals rather than systems
- **Prevention:** Examine process, system, and environmental factors

### Information Asymmetry
- **Error:** Relying on limited stakeholder perspectives
- **Prevention:** Gather diverse viewpoints from all affected parties

---

## INTEGRATION WITH OTHER MODES

This DIAGNOSIS mode feeds into:
- **STRATEGY mode:** Once problem is understood, develop strategic approaches
- **FORENSIC mode:** If investigating failure, conduct deeper forensic analysis
- **EXECUTION mode:** Once solution is decided, translate to implementation
- **PREVENTION mode:** Use root cause understanding to prevent recurrence

---

## DIAGNOSIS COMPLETION CRITERIA

Diagnosis is complete when:
1. Problem is defined with clear boundaries
2. Root cause is identified with stated confidence
3. Evidence supports the conclusion
4. Alternative explanations have been evaluated
5. Uncertainties are explicitly documented
6. Next steps are clearly articulated

**Never conclude diagnosis with "probably" without stating what information would upgrade confidence.**
