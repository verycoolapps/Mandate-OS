# CRISIS MANAGEMENT WORKFLOW

**MANDATE 360° Professional Analysis Protocol — Mode: CRISIS**

---

## PURPOSE

Provide immediate, structured response to urgent situations requiring rapid stabilization, containment, and recovery. This workflow prioritizes speed and decisive action while maintaining professional judgment and preventing escalation.

---

## CRISIS PROTOCOL

### Phase 1: Immediate Assessment (Minutes 0-15)

#### 1.1 Crisis Classification
- **Severity Level:**
  - **Level 1 (Critical):** Immediate threat to survival, requires all-hands response
  - **Level 2 (Severe):** Major operational impact, requires rapid response
  - **Level 3 (Moderate):** Significant disruption, requires coordinated response
  - **Level 4 (Limited):** Contained issue, requires standard response

- **Crisis Type:**
  - **Financial:** Cash crisis, fraud, market collapse
  - **Operational:** System failure, supply chain disruption, production halt
  - **Reputational:** PR disaster, data breach, executive misconduct
  - **Legal:** Lawsuit, regulatory action, compliance failure
  - **Safety:** Physical threat, cybersecurity attack, data breach
  - **Strategic:** Competitive threat, market disruption, technology obsolescence

#### 1.2 Immediate Impact Assessment
- What is happening right now?
- What is getting worse?
- What is at immediate risk?
- Who is being affected?
- What is the timeline for further damage?

#### 1.3 Stakeholder Notification
- **Who needs to know immediately:**
  - Decision-makers with authority
  - Operational teams directly affected
  - Legal and compliance (if applicable)
  - Communications (for external response)
  - Security (if physical or cyber threat)

- **Notification protocol:**
  - What to communicate
  - What not to communicate yet
  - Who is authorized to speak
  - What information is confirmed vs. uncertain

---

### Phase 2: Stabilization (Minutes 15-60)

#### 2.1 Immediate Actions
- **Stop the bleeding:** What action immediately stops further damage?
- **Containment:** How do we prevent spread or escalation?
- **Protection:** What must be protected right now?
- **Preservation:** What evidence or assets must be preserved?

#### 2.2 Crisis Command Structure
- **Crisis Lead:** Single point of decision authority
- **Functional Leads:** Operations, Communications, Legal, Finance, Security
- **Decision Frequency:** How often will leadership reassess?
- **Escalation Protocol:** When and how to escalate

#### 2.3 Resource Mobilization
- **People:** Who is needed immediately?
- **Budget:** Emergency spending authority
- **Systems:** What tools and access are needed?
- **External Support:** Legal counsel, PR firm, investigators, consultants

#### 2.4 Communication Control
- **Internal:** What employees need to know
- **External:** What stakeholders need to know
- **Public:** What public statement is needed
- **Silence:** What must not be disclosed yet

---

### Phase 3: Root Cause Investigation (Hours 1-24)

#### 3.1 Fact Collection
- What actually happened?
- When did it start?
- What systems or processes failed?
- Who was involved?
- What warning signs were missed?

#### 3.2 Evidence Preservation
- Log files, system records, communications
- Financial transactions
- Physical evidence
- Witness statements
- External communications

#### 3.3 Hypothesis Generation
- What are the possible causes?
- Which hypotheses are most likely?
- What evidence supports each hypothesis?
- What evidence contradicts each hypothesis?

#### 3.4 Preliminary Attribution
- **Internal failure:** Process, system, or people failure
- **External attack:** Malicious actor, competitor, or adversary
- **Environmental factor:** Market condition, natural disaster, external event
- **Mixed cause:** Combination of factors

---

### Phase 4: Recovery Planning (Hours 24-72)

#### 4.1 Recovery Hierarchy
- **Immediate:** Restore critical functions
- **Short-term:** Return to operational baseline
- **Medium-term:** Address root causes
- **Long-term:** Prevent recurrence

#### 4.2 Recovery Options
- **Option A:** [Description] — [Timeline] — [Cost] — [Risk]
- **Option B:** [Description] — [Timeline] — [Cost] — [Risk]
- **Option C:** [Description] — [Timeline] — [Cost] — [Risk]

#### 4.3 Resource Requirements
- What resources are needed for recovery?
- What can be reprioritized or postponed?
- What external support is required?
- What is the total cost of recovery?

#### 4.4 Recovery Success Criteria
- What does "recovered" look like?
- How will we know recovery is complete?
- What metrics will measure progress?
- What is the recovery timeline?

---

### Phase 5: Stakeholder Management (Ongoing)

#### 5.1 Communication Strategy
- **Audience Segmentation:** Who needs what information
- **Message Development:** What each audience needs to hear
- **Channel Selection:** How and when to communicate
- **Message Consistency:** Ensure aligned messaging

#### 5.2 External Stakeholders
- **Customers:** Impact communication and service updates
- **Investors:** Financial impact and recovery plan
- **Regulators:** Compliance and cooperation
- **Media:** Narrative control and transparency
- **Partners:** Supply chain and ecosystem impact

#### 5.3 Internal Stakeholders
- **Employees:** Situation updates and role clarity
- **Leadership:** Decision support and escalation
- **Board:** Risk exposure and governance implications
- **Shareholders:** Material impact disclosure

---

### Phase 6: Risk Mitigation (Hours 24-72)

#### 6.1 Secondary Risk Identification
- What new risks has the crisis created?
- What risks are elevated by the response?
- What cascading failures are possible?
- What dependencies are now exposed?

#### 6.2 Mitigation Actions
- For each new risk: specific mitigation strategy
- Early warning indicators
- Contingency plans
- Owner and timeline

#### 6.3 Legal and Compliance
- What legal obligations exist?
- What regulatory notifications are required?
- What liability exposure exists?
- What privilege or confidentiality must be maintained?

#### 6.4 Insurance and Financial
- What insurance coverage applies?
- What financial reserves can be accessed?
- What credit facilities are available?
- What financial reporting is required?

---

### Phase 7: Decision Framework

#### 7.1 Decision Criteria in Crisis
In crisis, decisions are made under:
- **Time pressure:** Limited time for analysis
- **Information uncertainty:** Incomplete information
- **High stakes:** Significant consequences
- **Resource constraints:** Limited capacity

#### 7.2 Decision Heuristics
- **Preserve life and safety:** Always first priority
- **Protect the core:** What must survive
- **Maintain options:** Avoid irreversible decisions
- **Communicate transparently:** Build trust through honesty
- **Learn continuously:** Update understanding as new information arrives

#### 7.3 Decision Authority
- Who has authority for what decisions?
- What requires consensus?
- What can be decided unilaterally?
- What requires board or legal approval?

#### 7.4 Decision Documentation
- What decisions were made
- Why they were made
- What information was available
- Who made the decision
- When the decision was made

---

### Phase 8: Post-Crisis Analysis (Days 7-30)

#### 8.1 Timeline Reconstruction
- Exact sequence of events
- Decision points and choices made
- Information available at each point
- Actions taken and their outcomes

#### 8.2 Root Cause Analysis
- Deep investigation using Five Whys, Fishbone, RCA
- System mapping of failure points
- Contributing factors vs. root causes
- Single points of failure

#### 8.3 Response Evaluation
- What was handled well?
- What was handled poorly?
- What decisions were correct?
- What decisions were incorrect?
- What information was missing?

#### 8.4 Lessons Learned
- What could have prevented this crisis?
- What could have reduced impact?
- What could have accelerated recovery?
- What systems or processes need to change?

- Full timeline

- Response evaluation

## COMMON CRISIS ERRORS TO AVOID

### Delayed Response
- **Error:** Waiting for perfect information before acting
- **Prevention:** Act immediately with available information, update as you learn

### Communication Chaos
- **Error:** Inconsistent messaging from multiple sources
- **Prevention:** Single communication lead with approved messaging

### Tunnel Vision
- **Error:** Focusing on one aspect while missing broader risks
- **Prevention:** Maintain broad situational awareness and secondary risk monitoring

### Blame Attribution Before Facts
- **Error:** Assigning blame before investigation
- **Prevention:** Focus on containment and recovery first, attribution later

### Overcompensation
- **Error:** Extreme reactions that create new problems
- **Prevention:** Calibrate response to actual severity and risk

### Information Hoarding
- **Error:** Withholding information from people who need it
- **Prevention:** Share information on need-to-know basis with clear rationale

## INTEGRATION WITH OTHER MODES

This CRISIS mode integrates with:
- **DIAGNOSIS mode:** Once stabilized, conduct proper diagnosis
- **FORENSIC mode:** Deep investigation after crisis
- **STRATEGY mode:** Address strategic implications
- **EXECUTION mode:** Implement recovery plan
- **PREVENTION mode:** Design prevention measures

---

## CRISIS COMPLETION CRITERIA

Crisis response phase is complete when:
1. Immediate threat is contained
2. Critical functions are restored
3. Stakeholders are appropriately informed
4. Secondary risks are managed
5. Recovery plan is in motion
6. Root cause investigation is initiated
7. Lessons learned process is established

**Crisis management is not complete until the post-crisis review is finished and prevention measures are implemented.**
