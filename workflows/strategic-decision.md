# STRATEGIC DECISION WORKFLOW

**MANDATE 360° Professional Analysis Protocol — Mode: STRATEGY**

---

## PURPOSE

Develop comprehensive strategic alternatives for complex decisions, evaluate long-term implications, and provide evidence-based recommendations with explicit trade-offs and opportunity costs.

---

## STRATEGY PROTOCOL

### Phase 1: Strategic Context

#### 1.1 Decision Frame
- What is the core decision to be made?
- What is the strategic objective?
- What is the decision timeline?
- Who has decision authority?
- Who must be consulted?

#### 1.2 Environmental Analysis
- **Market Context:** Industry trends, competitive landscape, market dynamics
- **Internal Context:** Organizational capabilities, resources, constraints
- **External Context:** Regulatory environment, economic conditions, technological shifts
- **Stakeholder Context:** Who is affected, their interests, their influence

#### 1.3 Strategic Alignment
- How does this decision align with organizational mission?
- How does it support long-term vision?
- What are the strategic priorities it serves?
- What strategic tensions does it create?

---

### Phase 2: Alternative Generation

#### 2.1 Strategic Options
Generate 3-5 distinct strategic approaches:

**Option A: [Name]**
- Description: What this option entails
- Rationale: Strategic logic behind this approach
- Requirements: What is needed to execute
- Timeline: Implementation duration

**Option B: [Name]**
- Description: What this option entails
- Rationale: Strategic logic behind this approach
- Requirements: What is needed to execute
- Timeline: Implementation duration

**Option C: [Name]**
- Description: What this option entails
- Rationale: Strategic logic behind this approach
- Requirements: What is needed to execute
- Timeline: Implementation duration

#### 2.2 Option Differentiation
- What makes each option distinct?
- Where do options overlap?
- Can options be combined?
- Are there hybrid approaches?

#### 2.3 Creative Alternatives
- What options are not being considered?
- What would a contrarian approach look like?
- What would industry leaders do?
- What would an outsider propose?

---

### Phase 3: Impact Analysis

#### 3.1 Financial Impact
For each option:
- **Revenue Impact:** Short-term, medium-term, long-term
- **Cost Impact:** Capital expenditure, operating expense, ongoing costs
- **Cash Flow Impact:** Timing of inflows and outflows
- **ROI Analysis:** Return on investment calculation
- **Payback Period:** Time to recover investment
- **Sensitivity:** How results change with key assumptions

#### 3.2 Operational Impact
- **Process Changes:** What workflows change
- **Resource Requirements:** People, technology, facilities
- **Capacity Implications:** Scale up or down
- **Capability Gaps:** What new capabilities needed
- **Transition Complexity:** How difficult to implement

#### 3.3 Strategic Impact
- **Competitive Position:** How market position changes
- **Differentiation:** What creates advantage
- **Moat Building:** What strengthens defensibility
- **Option Value:** What future options become available
- **Strategic Flexibility:** How constrained or flexible this makes the organization

#### 3.4 Stakeholder Impact
- **Customers:** Effect on customer value and experience
- **Employees:** Effect on workforce and culture
- **Partners:** Effect on ecosystem and relationships
- **Investors:** Effect on valuation and returns
- **Regulators:** Effect on compliance and risk

---

### Phase 4: Trade-Off Analysis

#### 4.1 What We Gain
For each option, identify gains:
- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Market share
- Competitive advantage
- Strategic positioning
- Capability development
- Risk reduction
- Option value

#### 4.2 What We Sacrifice
For each option, identify sacrifices:
- Capital allocation
- Management attention
- Organizational capacity
- Time and focus
- Existing capabilities
- Strategic flexibility
- Alternative opportunities
- Short-term performance

#### 4.3 What We Risk
For each option, identify risks:
- Financial exposure
- Execution risk
- Market risk
- Competitive response
- Regulatory risk
- Reputation risk
- Technology risk
- People risk

#### 4.4 Opportunity Cost
For each option, articulate:
- "By choosing this option, we cannot: [alternative opportunity]"
- What other initiatives lose resources
- What strategic options are foreclosed
- What capabilities remain undeveloped
- What markets cannot be pursued

---

### Phase 5: Scenario Analysis

#### 5.1 Base Case Scenario
- Assumptions: Most likely conditions
- Outcomes: Expected results under base assumptions
- Probability: Likelihood of this scenario

#### 5.2 Optimistic Scenario
- Assumptions: Favorable conditions
- Outcomes: Best possible results
- Probability: Likelihood of this scenario

#### 5.3 Pessimistic Scenario
- Assumptions: Adverse conditions
- Outcomes: Worst possible results
- Probability: Likelihood of this scenario

#### 5.4 Black Swan Scenario
- Assumptions: Unexpected catastrophic or transformative events
- Outcomes: Extreme results
- Probability: Low likelihood but high impact

#### 5.5 Scenario Comparison
- Which option performs best across scenarios?
- Which option has most predictable outcomes?
- Which option has highest downside risk?
- Which option has highest upside potential?

---

### Phase 6: Decision Criteria

#### 6.1 Primary Criteria
What matters most for this decision:
- Financial return
- Strategic alignment
- Risk level
- Time to value
- Resource requirements
- Stakeholder acceptance
- Competitive advantage
- Long-term sustainability

#### 6.2 Criteria Weighting
Assign relative importance:
- Must-have: Non-negotiable requirements
- Important: Significant factors
- Nice-to-have: Secondary considerations

#### 6.3 Option Scoring
Score each option against each criterion:
- Quantitative metrics where possible
- Qualitative assessment with rationale
- Evidence-based justification

---

### Phase 7: Recommendation

#### 7.1 Recommended Option
- Which option is recommended?
- What is the primary rationale?
- What evidence supports this choice?

#### 7.2 Confidence Level
- **High:** Strong evidence, clear advantage
- **Medium:** Evidence supports but some uncertainty
- **Low:** Limited evidence, significant uncertainty
- **State the basis for confidence assessment**

#### 7.3 Critical Success Factors
- What must go right for this to succeed?
- What are the key dependencies?
- What are the early indicators to monitor?

#### 7.4 Decision Gates
- What conditions must be met before proceeding?
- What additional information is needed?
- What approvals are required?
- What are the decision points along the way?

---

### Phase 8: Implementation Architecture

#### 8.1 Execution Roadmap
- Phase 1: [Timeframe] — [Key activities] — [Deliverables]
- Phase 2: [Timeframe] — [Key activities] — [Deliverables]
- Phase 3: [Timeframe] — [Key activities] — [Deliverables]

#### 8.2 Resource Requirements
- **Capital:** Investment needed
- **People:** Roles, skills, headcount
- **Technology:** Systems, tools, infrastructure
- **Time:** Implementation duration
- **Partners:** External vendors, consultants

#### 8.3 Risk Mitigation
- For each major risk: Mitigation strategy and contingency plan
- Early warning indicators
- Trigger points for course correction

#### 8.4 Measurement and Success Criteria
- **Leading Indicators:** Early measures of progress
- **Lagging Indicators:** Ultimate measures of success
- **Baseline:** Current performance level
- **Target:** Desired performance level
- **Threshold:** Minimum acceptable performance

## COMMON STRATEGIC ERRORS TO AVOID

### False Dichotomy
- **Error:** Presenting only two options when more exist
- **Prevention:** Generate at least 3-5 distinct alternatives

### Confirmation Bias
- **Error:** Seeking evidence that supports preferred option
- **Prevention:** Assign devil's advocate to challenge each option

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
- **Error:** Continuing investment due to past spending
- **Prevention:** Evaluate options based on future value only

### Overconfidence
- **Error:** Stating certainty where uncertainty exists
- **Prevention:** Use confidence levels and state assumptions

### Short-Term Bias
- **Error:** Prioritizing immediate gains over long-term value
- **Prevention:** Explicitly evaluate multi-year time horizons

### Groupthink
- **Error:** Suppressing dissenting views
- **Prevention:** Assign independent challenger role

## INTEGRATION WITH OTHER MODES

This STRATEGY mode connects to:
- **DIAGNOSIS mode:** Ensure problem is understood before strategy
- **SIMULATION mode:** Test strategic assumptions
- **INVESTMENT mode:** For capital allocation decisions
- **TRANSFORMATION mode:** For major organizational changes
- **EXECUTION mode:** Translate strategy to implementation

---

## STRATEGY COMPLETION CRITERIA

Strategic analysis is complete when:
1. Multiple viable alternatives have been generated
2. Each option has been evaluated on key criteria
3. Trade-offs and opportunity costs are explicit
4. Scenario analysis has been conducted
5. Recommendation is evidence-based with stated confidence
6. Implementation requirements are understood
7. Success criteria are defined
8. Decision authority is clear

**Never present a strategic recommendation without articulating what is being sacrificed and what opportunity cost is incurred.**
