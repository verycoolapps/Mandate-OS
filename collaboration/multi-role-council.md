# Multi-Role Council Protocol

## PURPOSE

The Multi-Role Council Protocol enables structured collaboration between multiple professional roles to analyze complex decisions from diverse perspectives, identify conflicts, synthesize consensus, and deliver unified recommendations with clear dissent positions.

---

## COUNCIL STRUCTURE

### Core Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|-----------------|
| **Moderator** | Manages council process, ensures all perspectives are heard, maintains professional discipline |
| **Lead Specialist** | Primary role with deepest domain expertise on the core issue |
| **Supporting Specialists** | Additional roles with relevant domain perspectives |
| **Independent Challenger** | Devil's advocate role — actively challenges assumptions and recommendations |
| **Risk Assessor** | Dedicated focus on identifying and evaluating risks across all proposals |
| **Decision Synthesizer** | Integrates all perspectives into final recommendation structure |

### Role Selection Criteria

- **Primary Issue Domain**: Determines Lead Specialist
- **Cross-Functional Impact**: Determines Supporting Specialists
- **Decision Stakes**: High-stakes decisions require Independent Challenger and Risk Assessor
- **Complexity**: Complex decisions may require multiple Supporting Specialists

---

## COUNCIL MODES

### 1. Single Role Mode
- **Purpose**: Deep analysis from one professional perspective
- **Use Case**: Domain-specific questions, specialized technical issues
- **Output**: Single-role analysis with standard professional output structure

### 2. Multi-Role Mode
- **Purpose**: Multiple roles analyzing independently, then synthesizing
- **Use Case**: Decisions affecting multiple domains, cross-functional initiatives
- **Process**: Parallel independent analysis → structured comparison → synthesis
- **Output**: Multi-role synthesis with agreements, disagreements, and resolution

### 3. Department Mode
- **Purpose**: Entire department perspective on organizational impact
- **Use Case**: Organizational changes, policy decisions, resource allocation
- **Process**: All relevant department roles contribute → department consensus
- **Output**: Department-level position with internal dissent documented

### 4. Executive Council Mode
- **Purpose**: C-suite strategic decision with highest-level perspective
- **Use Case**: Major strategic decisions, M&A, large capital allocation, transformation
- **Process**: CEO as Moderator, C-suite as specialists, structured debate
- **Output**: Executive council decision with individual positions recorded

### 5. Expert Review Mode
- **Purpose**: Deep domain expertise on specialized issues
- **Use Case**: Technical architecture, legal compliance, complex financial modeling
- **Process**: Subject matter experts conduct deep analysis → peer review
- **Output**: Expert consensus with confidence levels and knowledge gaps

### 6. Devil's Advocate Mode
- **Purpose**: Aggressive challenge to surface hidden assumptions and risks
- **Use Case**: High-stakes decisions, irreversible commitments, paradigm shifts
- **Process**: Independent Challenger actively questions every premise
- **Output**: Challenged analysis with stress-tested recommendations

### 7. Crisis Response Mode
- **Purpose**: Prioritized stabilization under time pressure
- **Use Case**: Active crises, emergencies, urgent operational failures
- **Process**: Rapid assessment → immediate action recommendations → follow-up analysis
- **Output**: Crisis action plan with containment, stabilization, recovery phases

### 8. Strategic Planning Mode
- **Purpose**: Long-term direction and scenario planning
- **Use Case**: Annual planning, multi-year strategy, market entry decisions
- **Process**: Long-term analysis → scenario modeling → strategic roadmap
- **Output**: Strategic plan with scenarios, contingencies, and milestones

## COUNCIL PROCESS

### Phase 1: Issue Framing

**Lead Specialist + Moderator**

1. Define the precise decision or problem
2. Identify decision scope and boundaries
3. Determine success criteria
4. Identify critical constraints
5. Establish decision timeline

**Output**: Issue Framing Document

---

### Phase 2: Role Assignment

**Moderator**

1. Identify all domains affected by the decision
2. Assign Lead Specialist based on primary domain
3. Assign Supporting Specialists for each affected domain
4. Assign Independent Challenger if decision is high-stakes
5. Assign Risk Assessor if decision involves significant risk
6. Assign Decision Synthesizer

**Output**: Council Roster with Role Mandates

---

### Phase 3: Independent Analysis

**All Specialists (Parallel)**

Each specialist conducts independent analysis following their role's professional mandate:

1. Apply role-specific intelligence matrix
2. Apply Universal Reasoning Protocol
3. Identify critical factors from role perspective
4. Generate role-specific recommendations
5. Identify risks and trade-offs from role perspective
6. Document assumptions and evidence requirements

**Output**: Individual Role Analysis Reports

---

### Phase 4: Structured Comparison

**Decision Synthesizer + Moderator**

Compare all role analyses on:

**Agreements:**
- Shared understanding of the problem
- Common risk identification
- Aligned strategic objectives
- Convergent recommendations

**Disagreements:**
- Differing problem definitions
- Conflicting strategic priorities
- Competing resource allocation preferences
- Divergent risk assessments

**Conflicting Objectives:**
- Short-term vs. long-term trade-offs
- Growth vs. stability tensions
- Innovation vs. operational reliability
- Revenue vs. customer experience

**Hidden Dependencies:**
- Cross-functional interdependencies
- Shared resource constraints
- Sequential implementation requirements
- Organizational capacity limitations

**Resource Conflicts:**
- Budget allocation disputes
- Talent allocation tensions
- Timeline conflicts
- Technical infrastructure competition

**Strategic Tensions:**
- Market positioning differences
- Competitive strategy disagreements
- Partnership strategy conflicts
- Go-to-market approach disputes

**Output**: Comparison Matrix highlighting agreements, disagreements, and conflicts

---

### Phase 5: Reconciliation

**All Specialists + Moderator**

For each disagreement or conflict:

1. **Surface the Assumption**: What underlying assumption drives the disagreement?
2. **Test the Assumption**: Is the assumption supported by evidence?
3. **Identify the Trade-Off**: What values or objectives are in tension?
4. **Explore Compromise**: Is there a solution that addresses both concerns?
5. **Document Unresolvable Differences**: If no compromise exists, document positions

**Reconciliation Outcomes:**
- **Consensus**: All roles agree on position
- **Majority Position**: Most roles agree, minority dissents with documented rationale
- **Unresolved Disagreement**: No consensus, requires escalation or decision-maker input
- **Compromise**: Middle-ground solution that partially addresses all concerns

**Output**: Reconciliation Report with resolution status for each conflict

---

### Phase 6: Risk Synthesis

**Risk Assessor + All Specialists**

Integrate risk perspectives from all roles:

1. **Financial Risks**: CFO, Investment Analyst perspectives
2. **Operational Risks**: COO, Operations Manager perspectives
3. **Technical Risks**: CTO, Engineering Manager perspectives
4. **Market Risks**: CMO, Market Researcher perspectives
5. **Legal/Compliance Risks**: Legal Counsel, Compliance Officer perspectives
6. **Reputation Risks**: PR Director, Customer Experience perspectives
7. **Security Risks**: CISO, Security Engineer perspectives

**Risk Classification:**
- **Probability**: Very Low, Low, Medium, High, Very High
- **Impact**: Negligible, Minor, Moderate, Major, Catastrophic
- **Time Horizon**: Immediate, Short-term, Medium-term, Long-term
- **Mitigability**: Easily Mitigated, Mitigatable with Effort, Difficult to Mitigate, Unmitigatable

**Output**: Integrated Risk Register with cross-functional risk view

---

### Phase 7: Decision Synthesis

**Decision Synthesizer + Moderator**

Create final recommendation structure:

1. **Executive Summary**: Clear recommendation with confidence level
2. **Council Composition**: Roles involved and their perspectives
3. **Analysis Synthesis**: What the council collectively determined
4. **Agreements**: Where the council is aligned
5. **Disagreements**: Where the council differs, with positions documented
6. **Final Recommendation**: Primary recommendation with rationale
7. **Alternative Recommendations**: Minority positions or alternatives worth considering
8. **Implementation Considerations**: Cross-functional implementation requirements
9. **Resource Requirements**: Budget, talent, timeline from all perspectives
10. **Risk Profile**: Integrated risk assessment
11. **Success Criteria**: Cross-functional success metrics
12. **Contingency Plans**: What to do if assumptions prove wrong
13. **Accountability**: Who owns which aspects of implementation
14. **Monitoring Requirements**: What to track and how

**Output**: Council Decision Report

## COUNCIL OUTPUT STRUCTURE

### Consensus Output

When all roles agree:

```
CONSENSUS RECOMMENDATION

Confidence Level: [High/Medium/Low]
Agreement Level: Unanimous

RECOMMENDATION:
[Clear, actionable recommendation]

RATIONALE:
[Shared reasoning across all roles]

IMPLEMENTATION:
[Unified implementation plan]

RISKS:
[Agreed risk profile]

SUCCESS CRITERIA:
[Aligned success metrics]
```

### Majority Position Output

When most roles agree but some dissent:

```
MAJORITY POSITION

Confidence Level: [High/Medium/Low]
Agreement Level: [X of Y roles]

MAJORITY RECOMMENDATION:
[Recommendation supported by majority]

MAJORITY RATIONALE:
[Reasoning from majority perspective]

MINORITY POSITIONS:

Role [Name]:
- Position: [Dissenting position]
- Rationale: [Reasoning for dissent]
- Critical Concerns: [Specific concerns]

RECONCILIATION ATTEMPTS:
[What was done to address minority concerns]
[Why compromise was or was not possible]

RECOMMENDATION WITH CONCERNS NOTED:
[Final recommendation acknowledging minority concerns]
```

### Unresolved Disagreement Output

When no consensus is possible:

```
UNRESOLVED DISAGREEMENT

Decision requires escalation to [Role/Decision-Maker]

DISAGRE POINTS:

Issue 1: [Description]
- Position A (Role X): [Position]
- Position B (Role Y): [Position]
- Source of Disagreement: [Underlying conflict]
- Evidence Requirements: [What would resolve]

Issue 2: [Description]
- Position A (Role X): [Position]
- Position B (Role Y): [Position]
- Source of Disagreement: [Underlying conflict]
- Evidence Requirements: [What would resolve]

RECOMMENDED ESCALATION PATH:
[Who should decide and with what information]

TEMPORARY RECOMMENDATION (if urgent):
[Interim position with clear disclaimer]
```

## COUNCIL DISCIPLINE

### Professional Conduct

1. **Role Integrity**: Each role speaks from their professional mandate, not personal preference
2. **Evidence-Based**: Positions must be supported by reasoning, not assertion
3. **Constructive Challenge**: Disagreement must be substantive, not rhetorical
4. **Listen First**: Understand opposing positions before challenging
5. **Document Assumptions**: Make implicit assumptions explicit
6. **Respect Expertise**: Acknowledge domain expertise when it exists
7. **Focus on Problem**: The goal is solving the problem, not winning the argument

### Common Fallacies to Avoid

- **Silo Thinking**: Ignoring other perspectives outside one's domain
- **False Consensus**: Assuming agreement without verification
- **Authority Bias**: Deferring to senior roles over evidence
- **Anchoring**: Fixating on initial positions
- **Groupthink**: Suppressing dissent to maintain harmony
- **Availability Bias**: Overweighting recent or vivid information
- **Confirmation Bias**: Seeking only information that supports one's position

---

## COUNCIL TRIGGERS

The council activates when:

1. **Multi-Domain Impact**: Decision affects three or more professional domains
2. **High Stakes**: Decision involves significant financial, operational, or strategic impact
3. **Complex Trade-Offs**: Decision involves significant competing objectives
4. **Irreversibility**: Decision is difficult or impossible to reverse
5. **Stakeholder Conflict**: Decision involves conflicting stakeholder interests
6. **Resource Competition**: Decision requires allocation of scarce shared resources
7. **Risk Profile**: Decision involves significant or uncertain risks
8. **Strategic Importance**: Decision affects long-term organizational direction

---

## QUALITY CONTROL

Before finalizing council output:

1. **Completeness**: Have all relevant perspectives been included?
2. **Clarity**: Is the recommendation unambiguous?
3. **Actionability**: Can the recommendation be implemented?
4. **Accountability**: Is it clear who owns what?
5. **Dissent Visibility**: Are minority positions clearly documented?
6. **Risk Transparency**: Are risks fully disclosed?
7. **Assumption Clarity**: Are assumptions explicit?
8. **Evidence Basis**: Are positions supported by reasoning?
9. **Implementation Feasibility**: Is implementation realistically achievable?
10. **Success Measurement**: Can success be measured?

---

## COUNCIL EFFICIENCY

### Time Management

- **Phase 1 (Framing)**: Immediate — defines scope for all subsequent work
- **Phase 2 (Assignment)**: Immediate — based on issue characteristics
- **Phase 3 (Independent Analysis)**: Parallel — scales with number of roles
- **Phase 4 (Comparison)**: Proportional to number of roles and issue complexity
- **Phase 5 (Reconciliation)**: Proportional to number of disagreements
- **Phase 6 (Risk Synthesis)**: Proportional to risk complexity
- **Phase 7 (Synthesis)**: Proportional to output complexity

### Scaling Guidelines

- **3-5 Roles**: Standard council, efficient process
- **6-10 Roles**: Larger council, requires structured moderation
- **10+ Roles**: Very large council, consider hierarchical sub-councils

---

## COUNCIL DOCUMENTATION

All council deliberations produce:

1. **Issue Framing Document**: Shared understanding of the problem
2. **Individual Role Analyses**: Each role's independent analysis
3. **Comparison Matrix**: Structured comparison of all perspectives
4. **Reconciliation Report**: Resolution of disagreements
5. **Integrated Risk Register**: Cross-functional risk assessment
6. **Council Decision Report**: Final recommendation structure

Documentation is retained for:
- Decision audit trail
- Learning and improvement
- Future reference for similar decisions
- Accountability tracking

---

## COUNCIL EVOLUTION

The council process improves through:

1. **Post-Decision Review**: Was the recommendation correct? Why or why not?
2. **Process Evaluation**: Did the council process work efficiently?
3. **Role Feedback**: Were the right roles included?
4. **Conflict Pattern Analysis**: What types of conflicts recur?
5. **Reconciliation Effectiveness**: How often were disagreements resolved?
