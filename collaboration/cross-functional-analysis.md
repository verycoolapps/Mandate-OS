# Cross-Functional Analysis Protocol

## PURPOSE

The Cross-Functional Analysis Protocol identifies and evaluates interdependencies between professional domains, surfaces hidden connections, resolves conflicts, and synthesizes integrated understanding of complex organizational decisions.

---

## PRINCIPLES

### 1. No Decision Is Truly Single-Domain
Every significant decision affects multiple domains. A product launch is not just a product decision — it affects finance, marketing, operations, legal, customer success, and more.

### 2. Interdependencies Are Often Hidden
The most critical dependencies are rarely obvious. Engineering decisions affect customer experience. Legal constraints impact product design. Financial limitations influence technical architecture.

### 3. Conflicts Reveal Trade-Offs
When domains disagree, the disagreement reveals a fundamental trade-off that must be consciously managed, not ignored.

### 4. Synthesis Requires Deep Understanding
True integration requires understanding each domain's professional mandate, not just acknowledging their existence.

## ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

### Step 1: Domain Mapping

For any decision, identify all affected domains:

**Primary Domain**: The domain that owns the core decision
**Secondary Domains**: Domains directly affected by the decision
**Tertiary Domains**: Domains indirectly affected through interdependencies

**Example**: New subscription product launch
- Primary: Product
- Secondary: Technology, Finance, Marketing, Operations, Legal
- Tertiary: Customer Experience, Sales, HR, Security

---

### Step 2: Interdependency Identification

For each domain pair, identify:

**Direct Dependencies**: One domain's output is another's input
**Shared Resources**: Domains compete for the same resources
**Sequential Dependencies**: One domain must complete before another can proceed
**Parallel Dependencies**: Domains must work simultaneously
**Constraint Dependencies**: One domain's constraints limit another's options

**Example**: Product ↔ Technology
- Direct: Product requirements → Technical architecture
- Shared: Engineering talent
- Sequential: Product specification → Technical design → Implementation
- Constraint: Technical feasibility limits product features

---

### Step 3: Conflict Detection

Identify where domain objectives conflict:

**Short-Term vs. Long-Term**: Finance wants immediate ROI, Product wants long-term market position
**Growth vs. Stability**: Marketing wants rapid expansion, Operations wants controlled scale
**Innovation vs. Reliability**: Technology wants cutting-edge, Operations wants proven solutions
**Revenue vs. Customer Experience**: Sales wants aggressive pricing, CX wants value preservation
**Speed vs. Quality**: Product wants fast iteration, QA wants thorough testing

**Conflict Classification**:
- **Resolvable**: Compromise or sequencing can address both concerns
- **Tension-Based**: Ongoing management required, no permanent resolution
- **Fundamental**: Core values or strategic choices in conflict
- **Resource-Based**: Competition for limited resources

---

### Step 4: Hidden Dependency Discovery

Many dependencies are not obvious. Probe for:

**Second-Order Effects**: What happens after the immediate effect?
**Upstream Dependencies**: What upstream changes enable this decision?
**Downstream Consequences**: What downstream systems will this affect?
**Lateral Dependencies**: What peer systems interact with this?
**Temporal Dependencies**: How does timing affect dependencies?
**Organizational Dependencies**: What organizational structures enable or constrain?
**Cultural Dependencies**: What cultural factors affect success or failure?

**Discovery Questions**:
- What has to change elsewhere for this to work?
- What breaks if this doesn't work?
- Who else needs to know about this?
- What assumptions are we making about other domains?
- What information do we need from other domains?
- What constraints from other domains are we ignoring?

---

### Step 5: Impact Propagation Analysis

Map how impacts propagate across domains:

**Direct Impact**: Immediate effect on primary domain
**First-Order Propagation**: Effect on directly connected domains
**Second-Order Propagation**: Effect on domains connected to first-order domains
**Systemic Impact**: Effect on the overall organization

**Example**: Major system architecture change
- Direct: Technology (new architecture)
- First-Order: Operations (new operational processes), Product (new capabilities)
- Second-Order: Customer Experience (new customer journey), Finance (new cost structure)
- Systemic: Overall organizational capability and competitiveness

---

### Step 6: Resource Competition Analysis

Identify where domains compete for shared resources:

**Budget**: Which initiatives get funding?
**Talent**: Which projects get the best people?
**Time**: Which priorities get attention?
**Management Attention**: Which initiatives get leadership focus?
**Infrastructure**: Which systems get capacity?
**Vendor Relationships**: Which partnerships get investment?

**Resource Allocation Framework**:
- **Critical Path**: Resources required for highest-priority initiatives
- **Shared Pool**: Resources that can be flexibly allocated
- **Dedicated Resources**: Resources permanently assigned to specific domains
- **Bottleneck Resources**: Scarce resources that constrain multiple domains

---

### Step 7: Risk Interdependency Analysis

Risks are rarely confined to one domain:

**Cascading Risks**: Failure in one domain triggers failures in others
- Example: Technical failure → Operational disruption → Customer dissatisfaction → Revenue loss → Reputation damage

**Compound Risks**: Multiple moderate risks combine to create severe risk
- Example: Technical complexity + Timeline pressure + Talent shortage = High failure risk

**Systemic Risks**: Risks that affect the entire organization
- Example: Regulatory change affecting multiple domains simultaneously

**Shared Risk Factors**: Common underlying causes affecting multiple domains
- Example: Economic downturn affecting Finance, Marketing, Sales, Operations

## SYNTHESIS PROTOCOL

### Agreement Synthesis

Identify where domains agree:

**Shared Problem Understanding**: All domains agree on what the problem is
**Shared Strategic Objectives**: All domains align on high-level goals
**Shared Risk Identification**: All domains identify similar risks
**Shared Success Criteria**: All domains agree on what success looks like

**Synthesis Output**: Unified understanding where consensus exists

---

### Disagreement Synthesis

Identify where domains disagree:

**Problem Definition Differences**: Domains define the problem differently
**Strategic Priority Differences**: Domains prioritize different objectives
**Solution Preference Differences**: Domains prefer different approaches
**Risk Assessment Differences**: Domains assess risks differently
**Timeline Differences**: Domains have different urgency perceptions

**Synthesis Output**: Structured disagreement with positions and rationale

---

### Conflict Resolution

For each conflict, determine:

**Is this a real conflict?** Or a misunderstanding?
**What values are in tension?** What trade-off is being made?
**Can both concerns be addressed?** Through sequencing, compromise, or creative solution?
**If not, which concern takes priority?** Based on strategic objectives and constraints
**How do we manage the ongoing tension?** If conflict is structural

**Resolution Outcomes**:
- **Integration**: Solution addresses both concerns
- **Sequencing**: Address one concern now, the other later
- **Compromise**: Partially address both concerns
- **Prioritization**: Explicitly choose one concern over the other
- **Mitigation**: Accept the trade-off and mitigate the downside

---

### Hidden Opportunity Discovery

Cross-functional analysis often reveals opportunities:

**Synergy Opportunities**: Where domains can amplify each other's efforts
- Example: Marketing and Product collaborating on customer research

**Efficiency Opportunities**: Where shared efforts reduce duplication
- Example: Technology and Operations sharing infrastructure investment

**Innovation Opportunities**: Where domain intersection creates new possibilities
- Example: Finance and Product collaborating on new pricing models

**Risk Mitigation Opportunities**: Where one domain's capabilities reduce another's risks
- Example: Security and Technology collaborating on architecture design

## ANALYSIS TRIGGERS

Cross-functional analysis is required when:

1. **Multi-Domain Decision**: Decision involves three or more domains
2. **High Interdependency**: Decision involves systems with many interconnections
3. **Resource Competition**: Decision requires allocation of scarce shared resources
4. **Significant Change**: Decision represents major organizational change
5. **Risk Cascading Potential**: Decision failure could cascade across domains
6. **Strategic Importance**: Decision affects long-term organizational direction
7. **Stakeholder Complexity**: Decision involves many stakeholder groups
8. **Regulatory Impact**: Decision has significant legal or compliance implications

## ANALYSIS DISCIPLINE

### Common Pitfalls

- **Silo Analysis**: Analyzing domains in isolation without considering interdependencies
- **Obvious Dependencies Only**: Missing hidden or second-order dependencies
- **Ignoring Tensions**: Pretending conflicts don't exist to maintain harmony
- **Surface Understanding**: Not deeply understanding each domain's professional mandate
- **Linear Thinking**: Assuming effects propagate in simple, predictable ways
- **Resource Blindness**: Ignoring competition for shared resources
- **Risk Isolation**: Treating risks as domain-specific rather than systemic

### Best Practices

- **Map First**: Always map domains and dependencies before deep analysis
- **Probe Deep**: Ask "what else?" and "what then?" repeatedly
- **Make Assumptions Explicit**: Document assumptions about other domains
- **Validate with Domain Experts**: Confirm understanding with domain specialists
- **Consider Time**: Dependencies and impacts change over time
- **Think Systemically**: Consider the organization as an interconnected system
- **Document Everything**: Maintain a clear audit trail of dependencies and decisions
