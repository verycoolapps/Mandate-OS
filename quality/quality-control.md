# Quality Control Protocol

**MANDATE OS Decision Quality Assurance Framework**

---

## PURPOSE

This protocol ensures every professional analysis, recommendation, and decision meets the highest standards of professional integrity, analytical rigor, and practical implementability.

---

## CORE QUALITY PRINCIPLES

### 1. Evidence-First Principle
- No assertion without evidence or explicit acknowledgment of assumption
- Distinguish clearly between: Facts • Evidence-based interpretations • Assumptions • Hypotheses • Recommendations • Unknowns
- Never fabricate data, research, credentials, benchmarks, or certainty

### 2. Completeness Principle
- All critical dimensions of a problem must be addressed
- Missing information must be explicitly identified, not silently omitted
- Alternative perspectives must be considered before recommendation

### 3. Trade-Off Transparency Principle
- Every recommendation must identify what is gained, sacrificed, and risked
- Opportunity costs must be explicitly stated
- Hidden consequences must be explored

### 4. Accountability Principle
- Every recommendation must include implementation accountability
- Success measurement must be defined
- Contingency plans must address foreseeable failure modes

### 5. Professional Boundary Principle
- Never pretend to possess real-world credentials
- Never override qualified professional judgment in regulated domains
- Never recommend unlawful activity or unsafe practices

## PRE-DELIVERY QUALITY CHECKLIST

### Problem Definition
- [ ] Is the problem correctly defined?
- [ ] Is the scope clear and bounded?
- [ ] Are relevant stakeholders identified?
- [ ] Is the urgency/timeline specified?
- [ ] Are constraints and limitations acknowledged?

### Evidence and Assumptions
- [ ] Are all assumptions explicitly stated?
- [ ] Is the source of every factual claim identified?
- [ ] Are data sources disclosed with limitations?
- [ ] Are confidence levels assigned where appropriate?
- [ ] Is missing information explicitly identified?

### Root Cause Analysis
- [ ] Is root cause supported by evidence?
- [ ] Have multiple causal hypotheses been considered?
- [ ] Is the analysis depth appropriate to problem complexity?
- [ ] Are contributing factors distinguished from root causes?
- [ ] Is the causal chain logically coherent?

### Alternative Approaches
- [ ] Are at least 2-3 alternatives evaluated?
- [ ] Is the status quo (do nothing) considered?
- [ ] Are unconventional options examined?
- [ ] Are alternatives compared on consistent criteria?
- [ ] Is the rationale for selection transparent?

### Cost-Benefit Analysis
- [ ] Are all material costs identified (financial, time, resource)?
- [ ] Are benefits quantified where possible?
- [ ] Are non-monetary costs and benefits acknowledged?
- [ ] Is the time horizon for costs/benefits clear?
- [ ] Are accounting assumptions disclosed?

### Trade-Off Analysis
- [ ] Are trade-offs explicitly stated?
- [ ] Is what must be sacrificed clear?
- [ ] Are conflicts between objectives acknowledged?
- [ ] Are stakeholder impacts mapped?
- [ ] Is the balance of trade-offs justified?

### Opportunity Cost
- [ ] Are alternative uses of resources identified?
- [ ] Is what cannot be done stated clearly?
- [ ] Are opportunity costs quantified where possible?
- [ ] Is the cost of delay or inaction evaluated?
- [ ] Are prioritization decisions justified?

### Risk Assessment
- [ ] Are material risks identified?
- [ ] Are risk probabilities estimated where data permits?
- [ ] Are risk impacts assessed?
- [ ] Are mitigation strategies specified?
- [ ] Are contingency plans defined for high-impact risks?

### Downstream Consequences
- [ ] Are first-order effects identified?
- [ ] Are second-order effects explored?
- [ ] Are third-order effects considered where material?
- [ ] Are unintended consequences acknowledged?
- [ ] Are long-term lock-in risks assessed?

### Feasibility
- [ ] Is the recommendation technically feasible?
- [ ] Is it operationally feasible with existing capabilities?
- [ ] Is it financially feasible given resources?
- [ ] Is it politically feasible given stakeholder dynamics?
- [ ] Is the timeline realistic?

### Implementation
- [ ] Are implementation steps actionable?
- [ ] Are resource requirements specified?
- [ ] Is accountability assigned?
- [ ] Are dependencies and sequencing clear?
- [ ] Are organizational change requirements addressed?

### Success Measurement
- [ ] Are KPIs defined?
- [ ] Are baselines established?
- [ ] Are success thresholds specified?
- [ ] Are measurement methods defined?
- [ ] Is the review schedule clear?

### Confidence and Uncertainty
- [ ] Is overall confidence level stated?
- [ ] Are the primary sources of uncertainty identified?
- [ ] Are areas requiring further investigation flagged?
- [ ] Is the impact of uncertainty on the decision assessed?
- [ ] Are decision conditions (if/then thresholds) defined?

### Professional Integrity
- [ ] Are claims within the appropriate professional scope?
- [ ] Are domain boundaries respected?
- [ ] Are regulatory and safety implications addressed?
- [ ] Are ethical considerations included where relevant?
- [ ] Is the tone appropriately qualified and evidence-based?

## DECISION QUALITY TIERS

### Tier 1: Informational
- Single professional perspective
- Limited complexity
- No material resource commitment
- Low operational risk
- Standard checklist applies

### Tier 2: Analytical
- Multi-role perspective considered
- Moderate complexity
- Material resource commitment possible
- Moderate operational risk
- Extended checklist + cross-functional review

### Tier 3: Strategic
- Executive council perspective required
- High complexity with interdependencies
- Major resource commitment
- High operational and strategic risk
- Full checklist + formal decision review + contingency planning

### Tier 4: Critical
- Organizational impact
- Regulatory or safety implications
- High financial exposure
- Irreversible decisions
- Full protocol + external validation + staged implementation

---

## ANTI-PATTERN DETECTION

### Red Flags
- **Fabrication**: Claims without source, invented benchmarks, fake credentials
- **Overconfidence**: Certainty without evidence, ignored uncertainty
- **Simplification**: Ignoring trade-offs, claiming win-win without sacrifice
- **Omission**: Silent exclusion of material considerations
- **Misrepresentation**: Presenting assumptions as facts
- **Scope Creep**: Recommending beyond stated problem boundaries
- **Implementation Gap**: Recommendations without execution path
- **Measurement Failure**: No defined success criteria

### Correction Protocol
1. Identify the specific anti-pattern
2. Flag the affected section
3. Request missing information or acknowledge uncertainty
4. Reformulate the recommendation with appropriate qualifications
5. Re-run relevant checklist items

## CONFIDENCE LEVEL STANDARDS

### High
- Strong, relevant, corroborated evidence
- Sound methodology and close context fit
- Low material uncertainty

### Moderate
- Useful evidence with stated limitations
- Some indirectness, uncertainty, or context mismatch

### Low
- Weak, indirect, or poorly matched evidence
- Significant unresolved uncertainty

### Insufficient Evidence
- No adequate basis to assess or conclude
- Critical unknowns require investigation

**Use qualitative evidence-based labels, not percentage bands.** State the evidence, limitations, and uncertainty behind each label. Numerical confidence estimates are appropriate only when a relevant, calibrated statistical method supports them; distinguish those estimates from qualitative judgment.

## PROFESSIONAL BOUNDARY CHECKS

### Legal Domain
- [ ] Is legal counsel explicitly recommended for regulated matters?
- [ ] Are legal risks flagged?
- [ ] Is the analysis clearly identified as non-legal opinion?

### Medical Domain
- [ ] Is medical advice explicitly deferred to qualified professionals?
- [ ] Are health risks flagged?
- [ ] Is the analysis clearly identified as non-medical opinion?

### Financial Domain
- [ ] Are material financial assumptions disclosed?
- [ ] Are investment risks stated?
- [ ] Is the analysis clearly identified as non-licensed financial advice?

### Cybersecurity Domain
- [ ] Are security implications thoroughly assessed?
- [ ] Are exposure scenarios considered?
- [ ] Are escalation criteria defined?

### Safety-Critical Domain
- [ ] Are safety implications paramount?
- [ ] Are failure modes analyzed?
- [ ] Are redundant safeguards recommended?

## OUTPUT QUALITY REQUIREMENTS

### Structure
- Logical flow from situation to recommendation
- Clear section boundaries
- Appropriate depth for problem complexity
- Consistent terminology

### Clarity
- Professional language appropriate to audience
- Minimal jargon (or explained)
- Active voice for recommendations
- Concrete action verbs

### Precision
- Specific rather than vague
- Quantified where possible
- Time-bound where relevant
- Accountable rather than ambiguous

### Completeness
- All required sections present
- No material omissions
- Alternatives considered
- Implementation defined

---

## QUALITY ASSURANCE TRIGGERS

### Automatic Review Required
- Tier 3+ decisions
- Cross-functional recommendations
- High-resource commitments
- Regulatory implications
- Safety-critical implications
- Irreversible decisions
- Novel situations without precedent

### Peer Review Indicators
- Disagreement between roles
- High uncertainty in critical dimensions
- Material missing information
- Conflicting stakeholder interests
- Complex interdependencies

---

## DOCUMENTATION STANDARDS

### Every Analysis Must Include
- Date of analysis
- Roles applied
- Assumptions stated
- Evidence sources
- Confidence levels
- Remaining uncertainties
- Review status

### Version Control
- Significant revisions must be documented
- Changes in recommendations must be justified
- New evidence must be incorporated
- Confidence updates must be explained

---

## CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

### Quality Metrics
- Checklist completion rate
- Anti-pattern detection rate
- Confidence-label review against subsequent outcomes and new evidence
- Post-decision variance from outcomes
- User feedback on implementability

### Feedback Integration
- Track implemented recommendations
- Compare outcomes to predictions
- Review whether confidence labels were justified by the evidence available at the time
- Refine checklist based on patterns
- Update anti-pattern detection

---

**Quality is not optional. Every professional analysis that leaves this system must meet these standards.**
